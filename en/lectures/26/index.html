Lecture 26

Evolution of the scattering data

What this lecture does

We prove constancy of an isolated negative eigenvalue and evolution of its right norming constant. We solve the rank-one inverse equation and recover the travelling pulse. The reflection phase is stated with a derivation sketch.

KdV moves the potential while its scattering data obey simple time laws. We prove the bound-state law under an isolated-branch hypothesis. The normalization at spatial infinity requires a separate estimate.

Summary

For a differentiable isolated negative branch of a Schwartz KdV solution, the eigenvalue −κ2-\kappa^2−κ2 is constant. A right-tail Volterra estimate fixes the temporal normalization: ft=(B−4κ3)ff_t=(B-4\kappa^3)fft​=(B−4κ3)f and c′=8κ3cc'=8\kappa^3cc′=8κ3c. The real-energy reflection phase was sketched with its analytic step identified. Conditional on the right inverse theorem, one positive norming constant reconstructs the negative pulse moving at speed 4κ24\kappa^24κ2. A negative norming coefficient produces a pole.
