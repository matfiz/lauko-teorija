Lecture 5

Domain of dependence

We prove an energy inequality on shrinking balls and deduce local uniqueness and finite propagation speed. A compact initial pulse and the heat equation show what the propagation statement does and does not say.

Lecture 4 constructs a field from data on all of space. The local energy balance gives a stronger conclusion: data far enough from a point cannot change the field at that point. We prove this for the real Klein–Gordon field with m≥0m\ge0m≥0 and propagation speed one.

Summary

For a real C2C^2C2 Klein–Gordon field with m≥0m\ge0m≥0, energy on a ball shrinking at unit speed decreases by its nonnegative lateral cone flux. Zero local initial data imply zero field throughout the cone, without a global energy assumption. Smooth approximation extends the inequality and local dependence to Lecture 4’s energy solutions. Compact initial support expands by at most ∣t∣|t|∣t∣. This conclusion allows interior dependence and does not assert Huygens’ principle. The heat equation has no such finite speed bound.
