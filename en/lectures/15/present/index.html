Lecture 15

Nonlinear local evolution

We solve the one-dimensional ϕ4\phi^4ϕ4 equation near a vacuum or a stationary kink. We prove the embedding and forcing estimates needed for contraction, then identify the only obstruction to continuing an energy solution.

A nonlinear equation need not have an explicit solution formula. We use the linear evolution to construct a nonlinear field by successive approximation. The construction gives a time interval shared by all data in a bounded ball.

15.4 Contraction and continuation

The lifespan T∗T_*T∗​ is the supremum of the times to which the unique solution with the given data can be continued in the energy class.

Proof. The path space XT=C([0,T];E)X_T=C([0,T];\mathcal E)XT​=C([0,T];E) is complete in the supremum of the linear norm, by completeness of E\mathcal EE. For two data in the same ball, subtract their integral equations and take the supremum norm. This gives ∥z−z~∥XT≤∥z0−z~0∥lin+12∥z−z~∥XT,\|z-\widetilde z\|_{X_T} \le\|z_0-\widetilde z_0\|_{\mathrm{lin}} +\frac12\|z-\widetilde z\|_{X_T} ,∥z−z∥XT​​≤∥z0​−z0​∥lin​+21​∥z−z∥XT​​, so the difference is at most twice the initial difference. Repeated short intervals also give continuous dependence on every compact interval on which the solutions remain in a common bounded ball.

15.5 Why the dimension matters

The embedding used above fails in higher dimensions. In three dimensions, let η(x)=e−∣x∣2\eta(x)=e^{-|x|^2}η(x)=e−∣x∣2 and uλ(x)=λ1/2η(λx)u_\lambda(x)=\lambda^{1/2}\eta(\lambda x)uλ​(x)=λ1/2η(λx) for λ≥1\lambda\ge1λ≥1. With I=(π/2)3/2I=(\pi/2)^{3/2}I=(π/2)3/2, substitution gives ∥uλ∥H12=I(λ−2+3)≤4I,∥uλ∥∞=λ1/2⟶∞.\|u_\lambda\|_{H^1}^2=I(\lambda^{-2}+3)\le4I, \qquad \|u_\lambda\|_\infty=\lambda^{1/2}\longrightarrow\infty.∥uλ​∥H12​=I(λ−2+3)≤4I,∥uλ​∥∞​=λ1/2⟶∞. Thus an H1H^1H1 bound cannot replace the supremum bound in that proof. This does not disprove cubic local existence in three dimensions: H1(R3)⊂L6(R3)H^1(\R^3)\subset L^6(\R^3)H1(R3)⊂L6(R3) controls a cubic in L2L^2L2, as developed on Sheet 15. In two dimensions the failure is logarithmic. The norm calculation integrates (1+r2)/r3(1+r^2)/r^3(1+r2)/r3 over 1≤r≤Λ1\le r\le\Lambda1≤r≤Λ. The value at zero uses ∫1Λdr/r=log⁡Λ\int_1^\Lambda \dd r/r=\log\Lambda∫1Λ​dr/r=logΛ. Answer. No. It disproves the H1→L∞H^1\to L^\inftyH1→L∞ estimate used here. The separate embedding H1→L6H^1\to L^6H1→L6 can control the cubic in L2L^2L2.

Summary

Subtracting a stationary vacuum or kink gives an H1×L2H^1\times L^2H1×L2 perturbation equation without a background force. In one dimension the Fourier embedding makes its cubic force locally Lipschitz from H1H^1H1 to L2L^2L2. The forced Klein–Gordon estimate gives a contraction on energy paths, uniformly on bounded data balls. Mild and weak energy solutions coincide. Uniqueness permits continuation, and any finite maximal lifespan forces the perturbation norm to tend to infinity.
