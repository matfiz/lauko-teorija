Lecture 23

KdV as a Hamiltonian field

What this lecture does

We prove the Gardner bracket’s Jacobi identity on a specified class of functionals. We derive KdV and its conservation laws, construct a travelling pulse and identify the obstruction to inverting its bracket operator.

The Korteweg–de Vries equation uses one real field and a third spatial derivative. We describe it by a Hamiltonian and a bracket whose fixed operator is spatial differentiation. This bracket also has conserved quantities that generate zero motion.

23.5 The Casimir and the derivative’s domain

A Casimir is an admissible functional whose bracket with every admissible functional is zero. The mass is a Casimir because {M,F}=∫RDxFu dx=0,XM=Dx1=0.\{M,F\}=\int_\R D_x F_u\,\dd x=0, \qquad X_M=D_x 1=0.{M,F}=∫R​Dx​Fu​dx=0,XM​=Dx​1=0. Its nonzero gradient lies in B\mathcal BB and generates zero motion.

The domain matters when discussing invertibility. On S\mathcal SS, the derivative has zero kernel: a constant Schwartz function must be zero. Its range is precisely S0={f∈S:∫f dx=0}.\mathcal S_0=\{f\in\mathcal S:\int f\,\dd x=0\}.S0​={f∈S:∫fdx=0}. Every derivative has zero integral. Conversely, for f∈S0f\in\mathcal S_0f∈S0​, the primitive h(x)=∫−∞xf(y)dyh(x)=\int_{-\infty}^x f(y)\dd yh(x)=∫−∞x​f(y)dy is Schwartz. On the positive half-line use h(x)=−∫x∞f(y)dyh(x)=-\int_x^\infty f(y)\dd yh(x)=−∫x∞​f(y)dy to estimate its rapid decay; the negative half-line uses the original formula. Its higher derivatives are derivatives of fff. Thus Dx:S→SD_x:\mathcal S\to\mathcal SDx​:S→S is injective but not onto. On B\mathcal BB, its kernel is the one-dimensional space of constants, and its range remains S0\mathcal S_0S0​.

The bracket therefore has a Casimir and no inverse on the full gradient space. A finite-dimensional Liouville–Arnold theorem cannot be applied to this infinite-dimensional degenerate bracket. Lecture 27 uses different structures to organise its conserved quantities.
