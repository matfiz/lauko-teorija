Lecture 9

Maxwell: variation and identity

What this lecture does

We fix the electromagnetic signs, vary the Maxwell action and derive charge conservation as a compatibility condition. We prove that gauge transformations leave the field unchanged and explain the resulting identity between equations.

An electromagnetic potential has four components, but its field equations cannot determine all four independently. We derive the equations from an action and identify the differential relation between them.

9.1 Potential and electromagnetic field

We work on R1+3\R^{1+3}R1+3 with metric η=diag⁡(1,−1,−1,−1)\Minkowski=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)η=diag(1,−1,−1,−1) and orientation vol=dt∧dx∧dy∧dz\vol=\dd t\wedge\dd x\wedge\dd y\wedge\dd zvol=dt∧dx∧dy∧dz. All fields in this lecture are real.

Definition 9.1 (Gauge potential and gauge transformation). An electromagnetic gauge potential is a C2C^2C2 one-form Aem=−ϕem dt+Ax dx+Ay dy+Az dz.\Aem=-\phiem\,\dd t+A_x\,\dd x+A_y\,\dd y+A_z\,\dd z.Aem​=−ϕem​dt+Ax​dx+Ay​dy+Az​dz. Its field strength is the two-form Fem=dAem\Fem=\dd\AemFem​=dAem​. A gauge transformation, with smooth scalar function χ\chiχ, is Aem⟼Aem+dχ,ϕem⟼ϕem−χt,A⟼A+∇χ,\begin{equation} \label{eq:9:gauge} \Aem\longmapsto\Aem+\dd\chi, \qquad \phiem\longmapsto\phiem-\chi_t, \qquad \Avec\longmapsto\Avec+\nabla\chi, \end{equation}Aem​⟼Aem​+dχ,ϕem​⟼ϕem​−χt​,A⟼A+∇χ,(9.1) where A=(Ax,Ay,Az)\Avec=(A_x,A_y,A_z)A=(Ax​,Ay​,Az​) is the spatial potential.

Recall from Topology and Geometry, Part I, that the exterior derivative satisfies d2=0\dd^2=0d2=0 and the graded product rule d(α∧β)=dα∧β+(−1)pα∧dβ\dd(\alpha\wedge\beta)=\dd\alpha\wedge\beta+(-1)^p\alpha\wedge\dd\betad(α∧β)=dα∧β+(−1)pα∧dβ when α\alphaα has degree ppp. Thus the transformed potential has the same field strength. This assertion uses equality of mixed derivatives, not a field equation.

Expanding dAem\dd\AemdAem​ defines the electric field E\EfieldE and magnetic field B\BfieldB by Fem=Ex dx∧dt+Ey dy∧dt+Ez dz∧dt+Bx dy∧dz+By dz∧dx+Bz dx∧dy,E=−∇ϕem−∂tA,B=curl⁡A.\begin{equation} \label{eq:9:fields} \begin{split} \Fem&=E_x\,\dd x\wedge\dd t+E_y\,\dd y\wedge\dd t+E_z\,\dd z\wedge\dd t\\ &\quad+B_x\,\dd y\wedge\dd z+B_y\,\dd z\wedge\dd x+B_z\,\dd x\wedge\dd y,\\ \Efield&=-\nabla\phiem-\partial_t\Avec, \qquad \Bfield=\curl\Avec. \end{split} \end{equation}Fem​E​=Ex​dx∧dt+Ey​dy∧dt+Ez​dz∧dt+Bx​dy∧dz+By​dz∧dx+Bz​dx∧dy,=−∇ϕem​−∂t​A,B=curlA.​(9.2) The spatial dot product and curl use the Euclidean spatial metric. Since dFem=0\dd\Fem=0dFem​=0, these fields obey div⁡B=0\divergence\Bfield=0divB=0 and ∂tB+curl⁡E=0\partial_t\Bfield+\curl\Efield=0∂t​B+curlE=0. For example, the coefficient of dx∧dy∧dz\dd x\wedge\dd y\wedge\dd zdx∧dy∧dz is ∂xBx+∂yBy+∂zBz\partial_xB_x+\partial_yB_y+\partial_zB_z∂x​Bx​+∂y​By​+∂z​Bz​. The coefficient of dt∧dy∧dz\dd t\wedge\dd y\wedge\dd zdt∧dy∧dz is ∂tBx+∂yEz−∂zEy\partial_tB_x+\partial_yE_z-\partial_zE_y∂t​Bx​+∂y​Ez​−∂z​Ey​. The other two coefficients follow by cyclic permutation.

9.2 The spacetime Hodge star

The action needs a map from two-forms to two-forms. We construct the needed constant-metric Hodge star here. This also recalls the convention of Topology and Geometry, Lecture 15. For basis wedges of degree ppp, the metric pairing is the determinant ⟨dxi1∧⋯∧dxip,dxj1∧⋯∧dxjp⟩=det⁡(ηiajb)a,b=1p.\langle\dd x^{i_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\dd x^{i_p}, \dd x^{j_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\dd x^{j_p}\rangle =\det(\Minkowski^{i_a j_b})_{a,b=1}^p.⟨dxi1​∧⋯∧dxip​,dxj1​∧⋯∧dxjp​⟩=det(ηia​jb​)a,b=1p​. Here x0=tx^0=tx0=t, x1=xx^1=xx1=x, x2=yx^2=yx2=y and x3=zx^3=zx3=z.

Definition 9.2 (Spacetime Hodge star). The Hodge star is the linear map from ppp-forms to (4−p)(4-p)(4−p)-forms determined by α∧⋆β=⟨α,β⟩volfor all forms α,β of degree p.\begin{equation} \label{eq:9:stardef} \alpha\wedge\HodgeStar\beta=\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle\vol \qquad\text{for all forms }\alpha,\beta\text{ of degree }p. \end{equation}α∧⋆β=⟨α,β⟩volfor all forms α,β of degree p.(9.3)

An increasing basis wedge pairs under wedge product only with its complementary wedge. Its metric norm is the product of its diagonal metric signs. These two facts determine its star uniquely and construct the map. For example, dt∧dx\dd t\wedge\dd xdt∧dx has metric norm squared −1-1−1, while (dt∧dx)∧(dy∧dz)=vol(\dd t\wedge\dd x)\wedge(\dd y\wedge\dd z)=\vol(dt∧dx)∧(dy∧dz)=vol. Hence ⋆(dt∧dx)=−dy∧dz\HodgeStar(\dd t\wedge\dd x)=-\dd y\wedge\dd z⋆(dt∧dx)=−dy∧dz. Applying the same rule gives all the entries needed below:

9.3 Action and its variation

Let the prescribed charge density ρ\rhoρ and spatial current J\currentvectorJ be smooth functions of spacetime. Define the current one-form and current three-form by j=ρ dt−Jx dx−Jy dy−Jz dz,J=⋆j,J=ρ dx∧dy∧dz−Jx dt∧dy∧dz−Jy dt∧dz∧dx−Jz dt∧dx∧dy.\begin{equation} \label{eq:9:current} \begin{split} \currentoneform&=\rho\,\dd t-J_x\,\dd x-J_y\,\dd y-J_z\,\dd z, \qquad \currentform=\HodgeStar\currentoneform,\\ \currentform&=\rho\,\dd x\wedge\dd y\wedge\dd z -J_x\,\dd t\wedge\dd y\wedge\dd z -J_y\,\dd t\wedge\dd z\wedge\dd x -J_z\,\dd t\wedge\dd x\wedge\dd y. \end{split} \end{equation}jJ​=ρdt−Jx​dx−Jy​dy−Jz​dz,J=⋆j,=ρdx∧dy∧dz−Jx​dt∧dy∧dz−Jy​dt∧dz∧dx−Jz​dt∧dx∧dy.​(9.4) Let D⊂R1+3D\subset\R^{1+3}D⊂R1+3 be a compact region, the closure of its interior, with the fields defined on an open neighbourhood of DDD. Only variations compactly supported in its interior will be used.

Definition 9.3 (Maxwell action). The Maxwell action with prescribed current is SD[Aem]=−12∫DFem∧⋆Fem+∫DAem∧J,Fem=dAem.\begin{equation} \label{eq:9:action} \action_D[\Aem]=-\frac12\int_D\Fem\wedge\HodgeStar\Fem +\int_D\Aem\wedge\currentform, \qquad \Fem=\dd\Aem. \end{equation}SD​[Aem​]=−21​∫D​Fem​∧⋆Fem​+∫D​Aem​∧J,Fem​=dAem​.(9.5) The current is held fixed when the potential varies.

Proposition 9.4 (Maxwell variation and differential identity). For a C2C^2C2 potential and every smooth compactly supported one-form variation aaa in the interior of DDD, δSD[Aem]a=∫Da∧(J−d⋆Fem).\begin{equation} \label{eq:9:variation} \delta\action_D[\Aem]a=\int_D a\wedge(\currentform-\dd\HodgeStar\Fem). \end{equation}δSD​[Aem​]a=∫D​a∧(J−d⋆Fem​).(9.6) Stationarity is equivalent to d⋆Fem=J\dd\HodgeStar\Fem=\currentformd⋆Fem​=J. The off-shell identity d(d⋆Fem)≡0\dd(\dd\HodgeStar\Fem)\equiv0d(d⋆Fem​)≡0 makes dJ=0\dd\currentform=0dJ=0 necessary for a solution. The action is invariant under every compactly supported gauge transformation exactly when dJ=0\dd\currentform=0dJ=0 in the interior of DDD. Gauge transformations leave the field equations unchanged and prevent them from determining the potential uniquely.

Proof. Since the metric is fixed, its star does not vary. The symmetry just proved and δFem=da\delta\Fem=\dd aδFem​=da give δSD[Aem]a=−∫Dda∧⋆Fem+∫Da∧J.\delta\action_D[\Aem]a =-\int_D\dd a\wedge\HodgeStar\Fem+\int_D a\wedge\currentform.δSD​[Aem​]a=−∫D​da∧⋆Fem​+∫D​a∧J. The graded product rule gives d(a∧⋆Fem)=da∧⋆Fem−a∧d⋆Fem\dd(a\wedge\HodgeStar\Fem)=\dd a\wedge\HodgeStar\Fem-a\wedge\dd\HodgeStar\Femd(a∧⋆Fem​)=da∧⋆Fem​−a∧d⋆Fem​. Thus the boundary contribution is the exact term −∫Dd(a∧⋆Fem)-\int_D\dd(a\wedge\HodgeStar\Fem)−∫D​d(a∧⋆Fem​). It is zero: extending its compactly supported coefficients by zero, each coordinate derivative integrates to zero by Fubini and the fundamental theorem of calculus. The remaining terms give (9.6). The pairing of one-forms with three-forms by wedge product is nondegenerate. Testing each coefficient with the fundamental lemma of Lecture 1 proves the stationarity assertion. Its smooth-test version follows by choosing a smooth nonnegative bump in a ball where the coefficient keeps its sign.

Applying d\ddd to the field equation gives dJ=0\dd\currentform=0dJ=0 because d2=0\dd^2=0d2=0. For a C2C^2C2 potential the coefficients of d⋆Fem\dd\HodgeStar\Femd⋆Fem​ are continuous. Its further derivative is interpreted distributionally, where mixed derivatives still commute. For a smooth potential the same identity holds pointwise. No equation for the potential was used to obtain the identity.

A gauge transformation leaves Fem\FemFem​ unchanged. Its exact change in the action is therefore SD[Aem+dχ]−SD[Aem]=∫Ddχ∧J=−∫Dχ dJ,χ∈Cc∞(int⁡D).\begin{equation} \label{eq:9:gaugechange} \action_D[\Aem+\dd\chi]-\action_D[\Aem] =\int_D\dd\chi\wedge\currentform =-\int_D\chi\,\dd\currentform, \qquad \chi\in C_c^\infty(\operatorname{int}D). \end{equation}SD​[Aem​+dχ]−SD​[Aem​]=∫D​dχ∧J=−∫D​χdJ,χ∈Cc∞​(intD).(9.7) Here the discarded exact term is ∫Dd(χJ)\int_D\dd(\chi\currentform)∫D​d(χJ). The fundamental lemma makes invariance for every χ\chiχ equivalent to dJ=0\dd\currentform=0dJ=0. Finally, adding any dχ≠0\dd\chi\ne0dχ=0 preserves the field strength and hence every field equation, which proves the nonuniqueness. 

9.4 Gauss and Ampère from the scalar density

Example 9.5 (The component field equations). Compute the Euler–Lagrange equations of the Maxwell action for ϕem\phiemϕem​ and A\AvecA. The Hodge table and the complementary wedge signs give Fem∧⋆Fem=(∣B∣2−∣E∣2)vol,Aem∧J=(−ρϕem+J⋅A)vol.\Fem\wedge\HodgeStar\Fem=(|\Bfield|^2-|\Efield|^2)\vol, \qquad \Aem\wedge\currentform=(-\rho\phiem+\currentvector\cdot\Avec)\vol.Fem​∧⋆Fem​=(∣B∣2−∣E∣2)vol,Aem​∧J=(−ρϕem​+J⋅A)vol. Thus the scalar density is L=12(∣E∣2−∣B∣2)−ρϕem+J⋅A.\Lag=\frac12(|\Efield|^2-|\Bfield|^2)-\rho\phiem+\currentvector\cdot\Avec.L=21​(∣E∣2−∣B∣2)−ρϕem​+J⋅A. The scalar potential occurs through E=−∇ϕem−At\Efield=-\nabla\phiem-\Avec_tE=−∇ϕem​−At​. Its density derivatives are ∂L/∂ϕem=−ρ\partial\Lag/\partial\phiem=-\rho∂L/∂ϕem​=−ρ and ∂L/∂(∂iϕem)=−Ei\partial\Lag/\partial(\partial_i\phiem)=-E_i∂L/∂(∂i​ϕem​)=−Ei​. For the spatial component AxA_xAx​, the nonzero derivative coefficients are ∂L∂Ax=Jx,∂L∂(∂tAx)=−Ex,∂L∂(∂yAx)=Bz,∂L∂(∂zAx)=−By.\frac{\partial\Lag}{\partial A_x}=J_x,\qquad \frac{\partial\Lag}{\partial(\partial_tA_x)}=-E_x,\qquad \frac{\partial\Lag}{\partial(\partial_yA_x)}=B_z,\qquad \frac{\partial\Lag}{\partial(\partial_zA_x)}=-B_y.∂Ax​∂L​=Jx​,∂(∂t​Ax​)∂L​=−Ex​,∂(∂y​Ax​)∂L​=Bz​,∂(∂z​Ax​)∂L​=−By​. 9.5 The identity and the source restriction

The components of (9.4) give the conservation condition dJ=(∂tρ+div⁡J)vol=0.\dd\currentform=(\partial_t\rho+\divergence\currentvector)\vol=0.dJ=(∂t​ρ+divJ)vol=0. To see the identity directly in the equations, set M0=div⁡E\mathcal M_0=\divergence\EfieldM0​=divE and M=∂tE−curl⁡B\boldsymbol{\mathcal M}=\partial_t\Efield-\curl\BfieldM=∂t​E−curlB. Commutation of spatial derivatives gives div⁡curl⁡B=0\divergence\curl\Bfield=0divcurlB=0, so ∂tM0−div⁡M=∂tdiv⁡E−div⁡(∂tE−curl⁡B)≡0.\begin{equation} \label{eq:9:identitycomponents} \partial_t\mathcal M_0-\divergence\boldsymbol{\mathcal M} =\partial_t\divergence\Efield -\divergence(\partial_t\Efield-\curl\Bfield)\equiv0. \end{equation}∂t​M0​−divM=∂t​divE−div(∂t​E−curlB)≡0.(9.9) For C2C^2C2 potentials this equality is distributional. On a solution, M0=ρ\mathcal M_0=\rhoM0​=ρ and M=−J\boldsymbol{\mathcal M}=-\currentvectorM=−J, giving charge conservation.

This identity also follows from gauge invariance of the source-free action. For a smooth potential, its variation along δϕem=−χt\delta\phiem=-\chi_tδϕem​=−χt​ and δA=∇χ\delta\Avec=\nabla\chiδA=∇χ is 0=∫D(−M0χt+M⋅∇χ) vol=∫Dχ(∂tM0−div⁡M) vol.0=\int_D(-\mathcal M_0\chi_t+\boldsymbol{\mathcal M}\cdot\nabla\chi)\,\vol =\int_D\chi(\partial_t\mathcal M_0-\divergence\boldsymbol{\mathcal M})\,\vol.0=∫D​(−M0​χt​+M⋅∇χ)vol=∫D​χ(∂t​M0​−divM)vol. Arbitrariness of the compactly supported function χ\chiχ forces the identity. For C2C^2C2 potentials the same integration by parts defines its distributional meaning. This is the first instance of Noether’s second theorem: a symmetry depending on an arbitrary function gives a differential relation between field equations. Lecture 19 develops the general statement.

Example 9.6 (An incompatible prescribed current). Take ρ(t,x)=t\rho(t,x)=tρ(t,x)=t and J(t,x)=0\currentvector(t,x)=0J(t,x)=0 on an open spacetime region. Its continuity expression is ∂tρ+div⁡J=1\partial_t\rho+\divergence\currentvector=1∂t​ρ+divJ=1. If a potential solved Maxwell’s equations there, the divergence of Ampère’s law would give ∂tdiv⁡E=0\partial_t\divergence\Efield=0∂t​divE=0. The time derivative of Gauss’s law would give ∂tdiv⁡E=1\partial_t\divergence\Efield=1∂t​divE=1. These contradict each other, even when interpreted distributionally. A nonconserved prescribed current is therefore inconsistent with these field equations. Lectures 10 and 13 require conserved sources.

Answer. No. Adding dχ\dd\chidχ leaves Fem\FemFem​ unchanged. The identity d(d⋆Fem)=0\dd(\dd\HodgeStar\Fem)=0d(d⋆Fem​)=0 holds off shell, distributionally for a C2C^2C2 potential. Conservation is necessary for the equations to have a solution.

Summary

The Maxwell action has density 12(∣E∣2−∣B∣2)−ρϕem+J⋅A\tfrac12(|\Efield|^2-|\Bfield|^2)-\rho\phiem+\currentvector\cdot\Avec21​(∣E∣2−∣B∣2)−ρϕem​+J⋅A. Compactly supported variation gives d⋆Fem=J\dd\HodgeStar\Fem=\currentformd⋆Fem​=J. The off-shell identity d2⋆Fem=0\dd^2\HodgeStar\Fem=0d2⋆Fem​=0 requires a conserved current. That condition also makes the source-coupled action gauge invariant for compactly supported gauge functions. Potentials related by dχ\dd\chidχ give the same field. Lecture 10 fixes this freedom when solving the initial-data problem.
