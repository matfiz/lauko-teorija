    Let P,M∈Cc∞(R1+3;R3)P,M\in C_c^\infty(\R^{1+3};\R^3)P,M∈Cc∞​(R1+3;R3) and define ρ=−div⁡P,J=∂tP+curl⁡M.\rho=-\divergence P,\qquad \currentvector=\partial_tP+\curl M.ρ=−divP,J=∂t​P+curlM. For its first moment, integrating each coordinate derivative gives ∫yiρ(t,y)d3y=−∫yi∂yjPj(t,y)d3y=∫Pi(t,y)d3y.\int y_i\rho(t,y)\dd^3y =-\int y_i\partial_{y_j}P_j(t,y)\dd^3y =\int P_i(t,y)\dd^3y.∫yi​ρ(t,y)d3y=−∫yi​∂yj​​Pj​(t,y)d3y=∫Pi​(t,y)d3y.   • Exercise 9.3 Vacuum duality with the Lorentzian star #

    For a vacuum Maxwell field, put F′=⋆FemF'=\HodgeStar\FemF′=⋆Fem​, using exactly the Hodge table of Lecture 9 and ⋆2=−1\HodgeStar^2=-1⋆2=−1 on two-forms.     Hint

    Express the starred two-form again in the basis dxi∧dt\dd x^i\wedge\dd tdxi∧dt and the spatial magnetic wedges. Their order matters.

    Short answer

    (E,B)↦(−B,E)(\Efield,\Bfield)\mapsto(-\Bfield,\Efield)(E,B)↦(−B,E); twice gives (−E,−B)(-\Efield,-\Bfield)(−E,−B) and four times the identity. Both examples have energy density 777 and flux (−6,0,3)(-6,0,3)(−6,0,3). For nonzero electric source, dF′=J\dd F'=\currentformdF′=J rather than zero.

    Worked solution

    The Hodge table gives ⋆(dxi∧dt)\HodgeStar(\dd x^i\wedge\dd t)⋆(dxi∧dt) equal to the corresponding spatial magnetic wedge, and the star of that wedge is dt∧dxi=−dxi∧dt\dd t\wedge\dd x^i=-\dd x^i\wedge\dd tdt∧dxi=−dxi∧dt. Thus Ei′=−BiE'_i=-B_iEi′​=−Bi​ and Bi′=EiB'_i=E_iBi′​=Ei​.
