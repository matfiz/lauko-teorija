Lecture 14

The Dirac field

We derive and solve the two-component Dirac equation in one space dimension. We prove finite propagation speed and identify its symplectic form on the underlying real solution space.

A field equation can be first order in both time and space. The Dirac field provides such an example. We construct its evolution and recover a conserved current and a real symplectic form. Its conserved positive norm does not make its classical energy positive.

14.5 The three answers in Part I

The constructions of Part I can now be compared on their stated domains.

Scalar Compact variations give Klein–Gordon. H1×L2H^1\times L^2H1×L2 data give unique causal evolution. Slice-independent form on compact-data solutions.

Summary

In 1+11+11+1 dimensions the two-component Dirac equation has unique unitary causal evolution for every L2L^2L2 datum. Its components solve Klein–Gordon distributionally, while its positive charge and indefinite classical energy are distinct conserved quantities on their stated domains. The action induces a real, nondegenerate, slice-independent form on the underlying real solution space.
