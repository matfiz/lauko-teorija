Radiation

What this lecture does

We derive the energy balance directly from Maxwell’s equations and prove the far-field power formula. We apply it to a slowly varying neutral dipole source and distinguish an absolute error from a relative one.

A compact source sends energy through increasingly large spheres. The observation time must increase with the radius to follow that energy. We compute the limiting outward flux of the retarded Maxwell field.

13.4 The limits of the source model

Retardation is a condition on the solution, as in Lecture 6. Adding a homogeneous Maxwell field preserves the source equations and can change the flux through infinity. Formula (13.5) describes the retarded field of the stated smooth source. The planned QFT course returns to choices of propagator and boundary conditions.

A point charge is outside these smooth-source hypotheses. The Abraham–Lorentz–Dirac self-force equation has runaway solutions. We do not derive or use it here. See Spohn, Dynamics of Charged Particles and their Radiation Field (2004), Chapter 9, and his study of the Lorentz–Dirac equation. The prescribed smooth current in this lecture is not a point particle acted on by its own singular field.

Summary

The Poynting vector is E×B\Efield\times\BfieldE×B and its balance includes −J⋅E-\currentvector\cdot\Efield−J⋅E. A smooth compact conserved current has retarded far-field power ∫∣n×A′∣2dΩ\int|n\times\mathcal A'|^2\dd\Omega∫∣n×A′∣2dΩ. Conservation removes the longitudinal electric coefficient.
