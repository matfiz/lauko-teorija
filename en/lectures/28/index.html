Gravity as a field theory

What this lecture does

We derive the Einstein equation using the Palatini identity, whose proof is sketched. We identify the boundary term and the covariant conservation identity. We separate the action calculation from the cited Cauchy theory and the further problem of constructing a reduced phase space.

The metric can itself be a dynamical field. Its action involves curvature, so it depends on second derivatives. We vary this action with the same inverse-metric convention used for matter in Lecture 3. Coordinate symmetry then gives a differential identity by Lecture 19.

28.1 Metric and curvature conventions

Let D⊂R4D\subset\R^4D⊂R4 be a compact coordinate region, with interior UUU. All fields below are smooth on a neighbourhood of DDD. Indices run from zero to three, and repeated upper and lower indices are summed.

Definition 28.1 (Lorentz metric and volume). A Lorentz metric here is a symmetric matrix field gμνg_{\mu\nu}gμν​ with one positive and three negative eigenvalues at each point. Its inverse is gμνg^{\mu\nu}gμν, defined by gμρgρν=δμνg_{\mu\rho}g^{\rho\nu}=\delta_\mu^\nugμρ​gρν=δμν​. With the coordinate orientation, its volume form is volg=∣det⁡(gμν)∣ d4x,d4x=dt∧dx1∧dx2∧dx3.\vol_g=\sqrt{|\det(g_{\mu\nu})|}\,\dd^4x, \qquad \dd^4x=\dd t\wedge\dd x^1\wedge\dd x^2\wedge\dd x^3.volg​=∣det(gμν​)∣​d4x,d4x=dt∧dx1∧dx2∧dx3.

We recall only the coordinate geometry used in the variation. The Levi–Civita connection is the torsion-free, metric-compatible connection with coefficients Γμνρ=12gρλ(∂μgλν+∂νgλμ−∂λgμν).\begin{equation} \label{eq:28:Gamma} \Christoffel^\rho_{\mu\nu} =\frac12g^{\rho\lambda} (\partial_\mu g_{\lambda\nu}+\partial_\nu g_{\lambda\mu} -\partial_\lambda g_{\mu\nu}). \end{equation}Γμνρ​=21​gρλ(∂μ​gλν​+∂ν​gλμ​−∂λ​gμν​).(28.1) For example, ∇μXν=∂μXν+ΓμρνXρ\nabla_\mu X^\nu=\partial_\mu X^\nu+\Christoffel^\nu_{\mu\rho}X^\rho∇μ​Xν=∂μ​Xν+Γμρν​Xρ and ∇μwν=∂μwν−Γμνρwρ\nabla_\mu w_\nu=\partial_\mu w_\nu-\Christoffel^\rho_{\mu\nu}w_\rho∇μ​wν​=∂μ​wν​−Γμνρ​wρ​. The product rule extends this to tensors, with one such term per index. Metric compatibility is ∇g=0\nabla g=0∇g=0. Our Riemann curvature, Ricci tensor, scalar curvature and Einstein tensor are Rρσμν=∂μΓνσρ−∂νΓμσρ+ΓμλρΓνσλ−ΓνλρΓμσλ,Ric⁡μν=Rρμρν,Rsc=gμνRic⁡μν,Gμν=Ric⁡μν−12Rscgμν.\begin{equation} \label{eq:28:curvature} \begin{gathered} R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} =\partial_\mu\Christoffel^\rho_{\nu\sigma} -\partial_\nu\Christoffel^\rho_{\mu\sigma} +\Christoffel^\rho_{\mu\lambda}\Christoffel^\lambda_{\nu\sigma} -\Christoffel^\rho_{\nu\lambda}\Christoffel^\lambda_{\mu\sigma},\\ \operatorname{Ric}_{\mu\nu}=R^\rho{}_{\mu\rho\nu},\qquad \Rsc=g^{\mu\nu}\operatorname{Ric}_{\mu\nu},\qquad G_{\mu\nu}=\operatorname{Ric}_{\mu\nu}-\frac12\Rsc g_{\mu\nu}. \end{gathered} \end{equation}Rρσμν​=∂μ​Γνσρ​−∂ν​Γμσρ​+Γμλρ​Γνσλ​−Γνλρ​Γμσλ​,Ricμν​=Rρμρν​,Rsc​=gμνRicμν​,Gμν​=Ricμν​−21​Rsc​gμν​.​(28.2) These are the recalled curvature conventions of Topology and Geometry, Lectures 16–17. In particular, Ricci and Einstein are symmetric.

28.2 Inverse-metric variation and the Palatini input

Vary the inverse metric by a symmetric array kμν=δgμν∈Cc∞(U)k^{\mu\nu}=\delta g^{\mu\nu}\in C_c^\infty(U)kμν=δgμν∈Cc∞​(U). For sufficiently small variation parameter, compactness preserves signature. As in Lecture 3, differentiating the inverse and using Jacobi’s determinant formula gives hμν:=δgμν=−gμαgνβkαβ,δvolg=−12τvolg,τ=gμνkμν.\begin{equation} \label{eq:28:determinant} h_{\mu\nu}:=\delta g_{\mu\nu} =-g_{\mu\alpha}g_{\nu\beta}k^{\alpha\beta},\qquad \delta\vol_g=-\frac12\tau\vol_g, \qquad \tau=g_{\mu\nu}k^{\mu\nu}. \end{equation}hμν​:=δgμν​=−gμα​gνβ​kαβ,δvolg​=−21​τvolg​,τ=gμν​kμν.(28.3) Indeed, δdet⁡g=(det⁡g)gμνhμν\delta\det g=(\det g)g^{\mu\nu}h_{\mu\nu}δdetg=(detg)gμνhμν​. The determinant keeps its sign for small variations, so the absolute value in the volume introduces no extra sign.

The variation of the connection is a tensor: it is the derivative of a difference of connections. Differentiating metric compatibility gives ∇μhνλ=δΓμνρgρλ+δΓμλρgνρ\nabla_\mu h_{\nu\lambda} =\delta\Christoffel^\rho_{\mu\nu}g_{\rho\lambda} +\delta\Christoffel^\rho_{\mu\lambda}g_{\nu\rho}∇μ​hνλ​=δΓμνρ​gρλ​+δΓμλρ​gνρ​. Add the two cyclic versions and subtract the third. Symmetry in the lower connection indices then gives δΓμνρ=12gρλ(∇μhλν+∇νhλμ−∇λhμν).\begin{equation} \label{eq:28:deltaGamma} \delta\Christoffel^\rho_{\mu\nu} =\frac12g^{\rho\lambda} (\nabla_\mu h_{\lambda\nu}+\nabla_\nu h_{\lambda\mu} -\nabla_\lambda h_{\mu\nu}). \end{equation}δΓμνρ​=21​gρλ(∇μ​hλν​+∇ν​hλμ​−∇λ​hμν​).(28.4)

We use the Palatini identity δRic⁡μν=∇ρδΓνμρ−∇νδΓρμρ.\begin{equation} \label{eq:28:palatini} \delta\operatorname{Ric}_{\mu\nu} =\nabla_\rho\delta\Christoffel^\rho_{\nu\mu} -\nabla_\nu\delta\Christoffel^\rho_{\rho\mu}. \end{equation}δRicμν​=∇ρ​δΓνμρ​−∇ν​δΓρμρ​.(28.5) Its proof is sketched here. At a chosen point take normal coordinates, where the background connection coefficients vanish. Varying (28.2) leaves the difference of the two derivatives of δΓ\delta\ChristoffelδΓ at that point; the varied quadratic terms vanish. The resulting expressions on both sides are tensors, so their equality holds in any coordinates. Sheet 28 supplies the complete tensor argument.

Contracting (28.5), using ∇g=0\nabla g=0∇g=0, gives a divergence: gμνδRic⁡μν=∇μvμ,vμ=gαβδΓαβμ−gμαδΓαββ=∇μτ−∇νkμν.\begin{equation} \label{eq:28:v} \begin{gathered} g^{\mu\nu}\delta\operatorname{Ric}_{\mu\nu}=\nabla_\mu v^\mu,\\ v^\mu=g^{\alpha\beta}\delta\Christoffel^\mu_{\alpha\beta} -g^{\mu\alpha}\delta\Christoffel^\beta_{\alpha\beta} =\nabla^\mu\tau-\nabla_\nu k^{\mu\nu}. \end{gathered} \end{equation}gμνδRicμν​=∇μ​vμ,vμ=gαβδΓαβμ​−gμαδΓαββ​=∇μτ−∇ν​kμν.​(28.6) For the last equality, substitution of (28.3) into (28.4) gives gαβδΓαβμ=−∇νkμν+12∇μτg^{\alpha\beta}\delta\Christoffel^\mu_{\alpha\beta} =-\nabla_\nu k^{\mu\nu}+\tfrac12\nabla^\mu\taugαβδΓαβμ​=−∇ν​kμν+21​∇μτ and gμαδΓαββ=−12∇μτg^{\mu\alpha}\delta\Christoffel^\beta_{\alpha\beta} =-\tfrac12\nabla^\mu\taugμαδΓαββ​=−21​∇μτ.

28.3 The Einstein–Hilbert action

Define the curvature integral by ID[g]=∫DRsc volg\mathcal I_D[g]=\int_D\Rsc\,\vol_gID​[g]=∫D​Rsc​volg​. Matter fields are held fixed in the metric variation. Assume their action has the Hilbert response of Lecture 3, δgSm,D=12∫DTμνkμν volg,\begin{equation} \label{eq:28:hilbert} \delta_g\action_{\mathrm m,D} =\frac12\int_D T_{\mu\nu}k^{\mu\nu}\,\vol_g, \end{equation}δg​Sm,D​=21​∫D​Tμν​kμνvolg​,(28.7) with a smooth symmetric tensor TμνT_{\mu\nu}Tμν​.

Theorem 28.2 (Einstein–Hilbert variation). Using the Palatini identity with the scope stated above, every compactly supported inverse-metric variation satisfies δID[g]k=∫DGμνkμν volg.\begin{equation} \label{eq:28:EHvariation} \delta\mathcal I_D[g]k=\int_D G_{\mu\nu}k^{\mu\nu}\,\vol_g. \end{equation}δID​[g]k=∫D​Gμν​kμνvolg​.(28.8) For GN>0\GN>0GN​>0, the Einstein–Hilbert action with matter is SD[g,matter]=−116πGNID[g]+Sm,D.\begin{equation} \label{eq:28:action} \action_D[g,\text{matter}] =-\frac1{16\pi\GN}\mathcal I_D[g]+\action_{\mathrm m,D}. \end{equation}SD​[g,matter]=−16πGN​1​ID​[g]+Sm,D​.(28.9) Its stationarity under all these metric variations is equivalent on UUU to Gμν=8πGNTμν.\begin{equation} \label{eq:28:Einstein} G_{\mu\nu}=8\pi\GN T_{\mu\nu}. \end{equation}Gμν​=8πGN​Tμν​.(28.10)

Proof. The scalar curvature varies as δRsc=Ric⁡μνkμν+gμνδRic⁡μν\delta\Rsc=\operatorname{Ric}_{\mu\nu}k^{\mu\nu} +g^{\mu\nu}\delta\operatorname{Ric}_{\mu\nu}δRsc​=Ricμν​kμν+gμνδRicμν​. Equations (28.3) and (28.6) therefore give δ(Rscvolg)=(Gμνkμν+∇μvμ)volg.\begin{equation} \label{eq:28:localvariation} \delta(\Rsc\vol_g) =\bigl(G_{\mu\nu}k^{\mu\nu}+\nabla_\mu v^\mu\bigr)\vol_g. \end{equation}δ(Rsc​volg​)=(Gμν​kμν+∇μ​vμ)volg​.(28.11) The coordinate divergence formula is ∇μvμ∣det⁡g∣=∂μ(∣det⁡g∣ vμ).\nabla_\mu v^\mu\sqrt{|\det g|} =\partial_\mu\bigl(\sqrt{|\det g|}\,v^\mu\bigr).∇μ​vμ∣detg∣​=∂μ​(∣detg∣​vμ). It follows by tracing (28.1) and applying Jacobi’s formula: Γρμρ=∂μlog⁡∣det⁡g∣\Christoffel^\rho_{\rho\mu}=\partial_\mu\log\sqrt{|\det g|}Γρμρ​=∂μ​log∣detg∣​. The vector density on the right has compact support in UUU because kkk and all its derivatives do. Extend it by zero outside DDD. Coordinate FTC and Fubini make its integral zero, without a boundary regularity assumption on DDD. This proves (28.8).

Combining this result with (28.7) gives δgSD=∫D(−Gμν16πGN+12Tμν)kμν volg.\delta_g\action_D =\int_D\left(-\frac{G_{\mu\nu}}{16\pi\GN} +\frac12T_{\mu\nu}\right)k^{\mu\nu}\,\vol_g.δg​SD​=∫D​(−16πGN​Gμν​​+21​Tμν​)kμνvolg​. The smooth-test fundamental lemma applied to each independent symmetric component gives (28.10). Conversely, this equation makes the displayed variation zero. 

The plus sign in (28.8) accompanies the minus sign in (28.9). Reversing only the action’s gravitational prefactor would reverse the relative sign of the sourced equation.

28.4 Coordinate symmetry gives the Bianchi identity

A diffeomorphism is a smooth invertible map with smooth inverse. Let ξ\xiξ be a smooth vector field compactly supported in UUU, and let FεF_\varepsilonFε​ be its local flow. Pulling the metric back by this flow produces its Lie variation. For the inverse metric it is kμν=£ξgμν=ξρ∂ρgμν−gρν∂ρξμ−gμρ∂ρξν=−∇μξν−∇νξμ.\begin{equation} \label{eq:28:lie} \begin{split} k^{\mu\nu}=\pounds_\xi g^{\mu\nu} &=\xi^\rho\partial_\rho g^{\mu\nu} -g^{\rho\nu}\partial_\rho\xi^\mu-g^{\mu\rho}\partial_\rho\xi^\nu\\ &=-\nabla^\mu\xi^\nu-\nabla^\nu\xi^\mu. \end{split} \end{equation}kμν=£ξ​gμν​=ξρ∂ρ​gμν−gρν∂ρ​ξμ−gμρ∂ρ​ξν=−∇μξν−∇νξμ.​(28.12) Metric compatibility gives the last equality. The curvature volume form is natural under pullback. Change of variables therefore makes ID[Fε∗g]\mathcal I_D[F_\varepsilon^*g]ID​[Fε∗​g] independent of ε\varepsilonε: the flow is the identity near the boundary. This is a gauge symmetry in the sense of Lecture 19. Its theorem permits second-order metric densities such as Rscvolg\Rsc\vol_gRsc​volg​.

Substitute (28.12) into (28.8). Symmetry of GGG and covariant integration by parts give 0=−2∫DGμν∇μξν volg=2∫D(∇μGμν)ξν volg.0=-2\int_D G_{\mu\nu}\nabla^\mu\xi^\nu\,\vol_g =2\int_D(\nabla^\mu G_{\mu\nu})\xi^\nu\,\vol_g.0=−2∫D​Gμν​∇μξνvolg​=2∫D​(∇μGμν​)ξνvolg​. The discarded divergence has current −2Gμνξν-2G^\mu{}_\nu\xi^\nu−2Gμν​ξν with compact support, so the same coordinate argument removes it. Arbitrary compact ξ\xiξ and the fundamental lemma imply ∇μGμν≡0.\begin{equation} \label{eq:28:bianchi} \nabla^\mu G_{\mu\nu}\equiv0. \end{equation}∇μGμν​≡0.(28.13) This contracted Bianchi identity holds for every smooth metric, without imposing the Einstein equation. It is precisely the formal-adjoint identity of Lecture 19 for the gauge operator (28.12). For a diffeomorphism-invariant matter action, the same argument including the matter variations gives ∇μTμν=0\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu}=0∇μ​Tμν=0 on the matter equations; this matter extension is stated here.

28.5 A nonzero constant-rescaling check

Example 28.3 (Constant inverse-metric rescaling). For a constant ε\varepsilonε, take kμν=εgμνk^{\mu\nu}=\varepsilon g^{\mu\nu}kμν=εgμν locally. This is a local check, not a compactly supported variation. Under gμν↦(1+sε)gμνg^{\mu\nu}\mapsto(1+s\varepsilon)g^{\mu\nu}gμν↦(1+sε)gμν, the covariant metric is multiplied by (1+sε)−1(1+s\varepsilon)^{-1}(1+sε)−1. Its constant factor cancels from (28.1), so the connection and Ricci tensor do not change. Thus Rsc(s)=(1+sε)Rsc(0),volg(s)=(1+sε)−2volg(0).\Rsc(s)=(1+s\varepsilon)\Rsc(0),\qquad \vol_g(s)=(1+s\varepsilon)^{-2}\vol_g(0).Rsc​(s)=(1+sε)Rsc​(0),volg​(s)=(1+sε)−2volg​(0). Differentiating their product at s=0s=0s=0 gives −εRscvolg-\varepsilon\Rsc\vol_g−εRsc​volg​. On the other side, gμνGμν=−Rscg^{\mu\nu}G_{\mu\nu}=-\RscgμνGμν​=−Rsc​ in four dimensions, so GμνkμνvolgG_{\mu\nu}k^{\mu\nu}\vol_gGμν​kμνvolg​ has the same value. Here τ=4ε\tau=4\varepsilonτ=4ε is constant and ∇νkμν=0\nabla_\nu k^{\mu\nu}=0∇ν​kμν=0, so the boundary vector vanishes pointwise.

28.6 Fixing the boundary value is not enough

The compact-support hypothesis in the theorem removes derivatives of the variation near the boundary. Fixing only its boundary value is weaker. Figure 28.1 shows a variation that vanishes on a flat face but has a nonzero normal derivative there.

Use the flat metric g=ηg=\Minkowskig=η on a rectangular region with x1≥0x^1\ge0x1≥0. Choose a smooth nonnegative, nonzero function a(t,x2,x3)a(t,x^2,x^3)a(t,x2,x3) compactly supported away from the tangential faces. Let χ(x1)\chi(x^1)χ(x1) be smooth, equal to one near zero and zero before the opposite face. Set k00=x1aχk^{00}=x^1a\chik00=x1aχ and all other components to zero. The entire array kkk vanishes on the boundary, but (28.6) gives τ=x1aχ,v1=−∂1(x1aχ)=−aat x1=0.\tau=x^1a\chi,\qquad v^1=-\partial_1(x^1a\chi)=-a\quad\text{at }x^1=0.τ=x1aχ,v1=−∂1​(x1aχ)=−aat x1=0. The outward coordinate flux on that lower face is −v1=a-v^1=a−v1=a. Its integral is nonzero, and all other faces contribute zero. Thus even fixing the metric on the whole boundary does not remove the Einstein–Hilbert boundary variation. The Gibbons–Hawking–York term is a boundary addition designed for the appropriate fixed-boundary-metric variational problem. Its formula and derivation belong to Sheet 28.

28.7 The three field-theory questions for gravity

The action question has the answer (28.9), with its variation proved using the explicitly sketched Palatini input. The Cauchy question requires constraints as well as evolution. For a precise cited case, take smooth data (Σ,γ,K)(\Sigma,\gamma,K)(Σ,γ,K) on a compact three-manifold without boundary, satisfying the vacuum constraints below. Here γ\gammaγ is a positive spatial metric and KKK is the symmetric tensor specifying its first normal change. Choose the convention Kij=−12∂tγijK_{ij}=-\tfrac12\partial_t \gamma_{ij}Kij​=−21​∂t​γij​ in coordinates with unit future normal ∂t\partial_t∂t​ on the initial slice. The vacuum constraints are Rγ+(tr⁡γK)2−∣K∣γ2=0,∇γj(Kij−(tr⁡γK)γij)=0.R_\gamma+(\tr_{\gamma}K)^2-|K|_\gamma^2=0,\qquad \nabla_\gamma^j\bigl(K_{ij}-(\tr_{\gamma}K)\gamma_{ij}\bigr)=0.Rγ​+(trγ​K)2−∣K∣γ2​=0,∇γj​(Kij​−(trγ​K)γij​)=0. The curvature and covariant derivative here are those of γ\gammaγ. Choquet-Bruhat, General Relativity and the Einstein Equations, Chapter VI, Theorem 7.4, pp. 164–165, and Theorems 8.3–8.4, p. 168, provide a local vacuum development of such data and geometric local uniqueness. A development is a spacetime solution realising γ\gammaγ and KKK on its embedded initial slice. Two developments have isometric neighbourhoods of that slice. This is a cited Cauchy result, not a consequence of the action calculation. It is a short-time statement, with no assertion of an infinite regular lifespan. The source uses the opposite spacetime signature; reversing the metric leaves the vacuum equation Ric⁡=0\operatorname{Ric}=0Ric=0 unchanged.

The phase-space question asks for gravitational data satisfying the constraints, modulo the appropriate diffeomorphisms. This is the idea of a reduced gravitational phase space. Its construction and any global manifold structure are not asserted here.

Self-check. For kμν=εgμνk^{\mu\nu}=\varepsilon g^{\mu\nu}kμν=εgμν in four dimensions, compute δvolg\delta\vol_gδvolg​ and GμνkμνG_{\mu\nu}k^{\mu\nu}Gμν​kμν.

Answer. The trace is 4ε4\varepsilon4ε, so δvolg=−2εvolg\delta\vol_g=-2\varepsilon\vol_gδvolg​=−2εvolg​. The Einstein trace is −Rsc-\Rsc−Rsc​, so the contraction is −εRsc-\varepsilon\Rsc−εRsc​. These are local rescaling identities, not a claim of compact support.

Self-check. Which signs combine to give a positive 8πGN8\pi\GN8πGN​ in the Einstein equation?

Answer. The curvature-integral variation is +∫Gμνkμνvolg+\int G_{\mu\nu}k^{\mu\nu}\vol_g+∫Gμν​kμνvolg​. Its action coefficient is −(16πGN)−1-(16\pi\GN)^{-1}−(16πGN​)−1, and the Hilbert matter variation is +12∫Tμνkμνvolg+\tfrac12\int T_{\mu\nu}k^{\mu\nu}\vol_g+21​∫Tμν​kμνvolg​. Setting their sum to zero gives the stated equation.

Self-check. Does the Bianchi identity require the Einstein equation? Which compact variation produces it?

Answer. No. It follows from invariance at every smooth metric. The inverse-metric Lie variation is kμν=−∇μξν−∇νξμk^{\mu\nu}=-\nabla^\mu\xi^\nu-\nabla^\nu\xi^\mukμν=−∇μξν−∇νξμ for arbitrary compact ξ\xiξ. Its formal adjoint gives ∇μGμν≡0\nabla^\mu G_{\mu\nu}\equiv0∇μGμν​≡0.

Self-check. For the flat-face variation k00=x1aχk^{00}=x^1a\chik00=x1aχ, why does k=0k=0k=0 on the boundary not make its boundary contribution vanish?

Answer. Its normal derivative remains nonzero: v1=−av^1=-av1=−a at the face x1=0x^1=0x1=0. The outward flux there is aaa. Compact support in the interior would remove both the variation and its derivatives near the boundary.

Summary

With inverse-metric variation, δvolg=−12gμνkμνvolg\delta\vol_g=-\tfrac12g_{\mu\nu}k^{\mu\nu}\vol_gδvolg​=−21​gμν​kμνvolg​. The Palatini input gives a divergence plus GμνkμνG_{\mu\nu}k^{\mu\nu}Gμν​kμν. Compact support removes that divergence and the stated action gives Gμν=8πGNTμνG_{\mu\nu}=8\pi\GN T_{\mu\nu}Gμν​=8πGN​Tμν​. Coordinate symmetry yields ∇μGμν≡0\nabla^\mu G_{\mu\nu}\equiv0∇μGμν​≡0 off shell. Fixing a boundary metric alone leaves a normal-derivative term. The Cauchy theory with constraints is cited, and the reduced phase space is named.
