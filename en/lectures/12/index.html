Lecture 12

Transverse reduction

What this lecture does

We construct the transverse projector, identify the null directions of the constrained canonical form and derive the reduced bracket. Constant fields on a torus show why zero frequency requires a separate analysis.

The Gauss constraint restricts Maxwell’s momentum. Gauge transformations identify different spatial potentials. We impose the restriction and make the identification explicitly, using Fourier transformation away from zero frequency. The remaining variables have two independent directions at each nonzero wave vector.

12.2 Projection and the quotient

Definition 12.3 (Transverse projector). For k≠0k\ne0k=0, the transverse projector is the matrix PT(k)=I−kkT∣k∣2,PT(k)ij=δij−kikj∣k∣2.\begin{equation} \label{eq:12:projector} \PT(k)=I-\frac{kk^{\mathsf T}}{|k|^2}, \qquad \PT(k)_{ij}=\delta_{ij}-\frac{k_i k_j}{|k|^2}. \end{equation}PT​(k)=I−∣k∣2kkT​,PT​(k)ij​=δij​−∣k∣2ki​kj​​.(12.2) For v∈X3v\in\mathcal X^3v∈X3, define its transverse part by vT^(k)=PT(k)v^(k)\widehat{v_T}(k)=\PT(k)\widehat v(k)vT​​(k)=PT​(k)v(k). The matrix projects a vector onto the plane perpendicular to kkk. Figure 12.1 separates this transverse part from the longitudinal part parallel to kkk.

For real smearing functions f,g∈X3f,g\in\mathcal X^3f,g∈X3, the reduced linear observables are AT(f)=∫f⋅AT d3x,ΠT(g)=∫g⋅ΠT d3x.A_T(f)=\int f\cdot\Avec_T\,\dd^3x, \qquad \Pi_T(g)=\int g\cdot\boldsymbol\Pi_T\,\dd^3x.AT​(f)=∫f⋅AT​d3x,ΠT​(g)=∫g⋅ΠT​d3x. Theorem 12.4 (Transverse reduction). In the stated annulus class, every orbit in C\mathcal CC has exactly one representative with div⁡AT=0\divergence\Avec_T=0divAT​=0. The quotient is identified with ZT={(AT,ΠT)∈X3×X3:div⁡AT=div⁡ΠT=0}.\mathcal Z_T=\{(\Avec_T,\boldsymbol\Pi_T)\in\mathcal X^3\times\mathcal X^3: \divergence\Avec_T=\divergence\boldsymbol\Pi_T=0\}.ZT​={(AT​,ΠT​)∈X3×X3:divAT​=divΠT​=0}. The kernel of the constrained canonical form consists exactly of the gauge directions (∇χ,0)(\nabla\chi,0)(∇χ,0). The form therefore descends to the nondegenerate canonical form (12.1) on ZT\mathcal Z_TZT​. The reduced bracket on smeared generators is {AT(f),ΠT(g)}red=∫f⋅PTg d3x,{AT(f),AT(g)}red={ΠT(f),ΠT(g)}red=0.\begin{equation} \label{eq:12:smearbracket} \{A_T(f),\Pi_T(g)\}_{\mathrm{red}}=\int f\cdot\PT g\,\dd^3x, \qquad \{A_T(f),A_T(g)\}_{\mathrm{red}} =\{\Pi_T(f),\Pi_T(g)\}_{\mathrm{red}}=0. \end{equation}{AT​(f),ΠT​(g)}red​=∫f⋅PT​gd3x,{AT​(f),AT​(g)}red​={ΠT​(f),ΠT​(g)}red​=0.(12.3) The bracket on their polynomial algebra follows by bilinearity and the product rule. At each k≠0k\ne0k=0, the transverse position and momentum each have two independent components.

Proof. First, put K=kkT/∣k∣2K=kk^{\mathsf T}/|k|^2K=kkT/∣k∣2. Since kTk=∣k∣2k^{\mathsf T}k=|k|^2kTk=∣k∣2, K2=KK^2=KK2=K. Thus PT2=(I−K)2=I−K=PT\PT^2=(I-K)^2=I-K=\PTPT2​=(I−K)2=I−K=PT​. Also PTT=PT\PT^{\mathsf T}=\PTPTT​=PT​, PTk=0\PT k=0PT​k=0 and tr⁡PT=3−1=2\operatorname{tr}\PT=3-1=2trPT​=3−1=2. These identities show that PT\PTPT​ is the orthogonal projection onto k⊥k^\perpk⊥. It is real and even in kkk, so it preserves Fourier reality.

The transverse part of a potential is gauge invariant because PT(A^+ikχ^)=PTA^\PT(\widehat\Avec+ik\widehat\chi)=\PT\widehat\AvecPT​(A+ikχ​)=PT​A. To reach that representative, choose χ^(k)=i k⋅A^(k)∣k∣2.\widehat\chi(k)=\frac{i\,k\cdot\widehat\Avec(k)}{|k|^2}.χ​(k)=∣k∣2ik⋅A(k)​. This is a smooth compact annulus profile with the required reality condition. It gives A^+ikχ^=PTA^\widehat\Avec+ik\widehat\chi=\PT\widehat\AvecA+ikχ​=PT​A. If two transverse representatives differ by ikχ^ik\widehat\chiikχ​, taking the dot product with kkk gives i∣k∣2χ^=0i|k|^2\widehat\chi=0i∣k∣2χ​=0. Thus they coincide. The constraint already gives Π=ΠT\boldsymbol\Pi=\boldsymbol\Pi_TΠ=ΠT​.

Next, a gauge direction pairs to zero with every (b,q)∈C(b,q)\in\mathcal C(b,q)∈C: Ω((∇χ,0),(b,q))=∫∇χ⋅q d3x=−∫χ div⁡q d3x=0.\symplecticform((\nabla\chi,0),(b,q)) =\int\nabla\chi\cdot q\,\dd^3x =-\int\chi\,\divergence q\,\dd^3x=0.Ω((∇χ,0),(b,q))=∫∇χ⋅qd3x=−∫χdivqd3x=0. Schwartz decay removes the face terms, as in Lecture 7. Conversely, suppose (a,p)(a,p)(a,p) is in the kernel. Testing against (p,0)(p,0)(p,0) gives −∫∣p∣2=0-\int|p|^2=0−∫∣p∣2=0, so p=0p=0p=0. Testing against (0,PTa)(0,\PT a)(0,PT​a) gives ∫a⋅PTa=∫∣PTa∣2=0\int a\cdot\PT a=\int|\PT a|^2=0∫a⋅PT​a=∫∣PT​a∣2=0, by symmetry and idempotence of the multiplier and Plancherel. For a transverse pair (a,p)(a,p)(a,p), its pairing with (−p,a)(-p,a)(−p,a) is ∥a∥22+∥p∥22\|a\|_2^2+\|p\|_2^2∥a∥22​+∥p∥22​. Thus the quotient form is nondegenerate. 12.3 Two polarisations and the Fourier bracket

A polarisation vector at k≠0k\ne0k=0 is a unit vector in k⊥k^\perpk⊥. Choose an orthonormal pair ε1(k)\varepsilon_1(k)ε1​(k) and ε2(k)\varepsilon_2(k)ε2​(k) at that fixed kkk. The two components of A^T\widehat\Avec_TAT​ along this pair, and the two corresponding momentum components, describe the transverse data there. This pointwise choice does not require a smooth basis on the whole annulus.

The Fourier transform of a real field couples kkk to −k-k−k through its reality condition. The sum k+k′k+k'k+k′ comes from the two forward Fourier phases: (2π)−3∫R3e−i(k+k′)⋅x d3x=δ(k+k′).(2\pi)^{-3}\int_{\R^3}e^{-i(k+k')\cdot x}\,\dd^3x=\delta(k+k').(2π)−3∫R3​e−i(k+k′)⋅xd3x=δ(k+k′). 12.4 The zero mode on the torus

The annulus assumption excludes harmonic data. On T3\torus^3T3, a constant vector field is harmonic: every component has zero Laplacian. Take constant data A=a\Avec=aA=a and Π=p\boldsymbol\Pi=pΠ=p, with a,p∈R3a,p\in\R^3a,p∈R3. The Gauss constraint holds automatically. Conversely, Δv=0\Delta v=0Δv=0 on the torus gives ∣n∣2v^n=0|n|^2\widehat v_n=0∣n∣2vn​=0, so all harmonic Fourier components have n=0n=0n=0 and are constant. A periodic gauge parameter obeys ∫T3∇χ d3x=0\int_{\torus^3}\nabla\chi\,\dd^3x=0∫T3​∇χd3x=0, by opposite-face cancellation. Consequently it cannot change the mean aaa. These constant modes survive the quotient, with canonical form Ωharm=(2π)3∑i=13δai∧δpi.\symplecticform_{\mathrm{harm}}=(2\pi)^3\sum_{i=1}^3\delta a_i\wedge\delta p_i.Ωharm​=(2π)3i=1∑3​δai​∧δpi​. There are three harmonic pairs. Applying the nonzero-kkk count to k=0k=0k=0 would incorrectly remove one of them. Here gauge parameters are single-valued periodic real functions, as in the gauge action above. Lectures 20 and 21 must retain harmonic and global data when the spatial topology changes.

12.5 The bounded projector and the energy-space scope

For any v∈L2(R3;R3)v\in L^2(\R^3;\R^3)v∈L2(R3;R3), define PTv\PT vPT​v directly by the multiplier (12.2) for k≠0k\ne0k=0. Its value at the single point k=0k=0k=0 has no effect on an L2L^2L2 function. Orthogonality gives ∥PTv∥22=∫∣PT(k)v^(k)∣2 d3k≤∫∣v^(k)∣2 d3k=∥v∥22.\|\PT v\|_2^2=\int|\PT(k)\widehat v(k)|^2\,\dd^3k \le\int|\widehat v(k)|^2\,\dd^3k=\|v\|_2^2.∥PT​v∥22​=∫∣PT​(k)v(k)∣2d3k≤∫∣v(k)∣2d3k=∥v∥22​. Thus this is a bounded orthogonal projector on all of L2L^2L2, not merely on one fixed annulus. The same calculation with Fourier weight 1+∣k∣21+|k|^21+∣k∣2 gives its bounded action on H1H^1H1.

The corresponding inhomogeneous energy-space reduction also follows from this projector. Take A∈H1\Avec\in H^1A∈H1 and Π∈L2\boldsymbol\Pi\in L^2Π∈L2 with div⁡Π=0\divergence\boldsymbol\Pi=0divΠ=0 distributionally, and quotient by distributional gradients ∇χ∈H1\nabla\chi\in H^1∇χ∈H1. The reduced space is Htrans1(R3;R3)×Ltrans2(R3;R3),H^1_{\mathrm{trans}}(\R^3;\R^3)\times L^2_{\mathrm{trans}}(\R^3;\R^3),Htrans1​(R3;R3)×Ltrans2​(R3;R3), where the subscript means distributional divergence zero. Indeed, set χ^=−i(k⋅A^)/∣k∣2\widehat\chi=-i(k\cdot\widehat\Avec)/|k|^2χ​=−i(k⋅A)/∣k∣2 for k≠0k\ne0k=0. This defines a tempered distribution: near zero, Cauchy–Schwarz uses ∫∣k∣<1∣k∣−2d3k<∞\int_{|k|<1}|k|^{-2}\dd^3k<\infty∫∣k∣<1​∣k∣−2d3k<∞ to prove local integrability; at infinity its pairing with every Schwartz test converges by Cauchy–Schwarz. Conversely, an H1H^1H1 gradient is longitudinal: its Fourier curl is zero, so k×∇χ^=0k\times\widehat{\nabla\chi}=0k×∇χ​=0 almost everywhere and its transverse projection vanishes. Thus each orbit has exactly one transverse representative. The canonical form descends because longitudinal gradients are L2L^2L2-orthogonal to transverse momenta, by Plancherel.

Its form (12.1) is weakly nondegenerate. If (a,p)(a,p)(a,p) pairs to zero with every transverse pair, testing with (0,a)(0,a)(0,a) first gives a=0a=0a=0. Self-check. At k=(0,0,2)k=(0,0,2)k=(0,0,2), project v=(3,−1,5)v=(3,-1,5)v=(3,−1,5). Which canonical components does the Gauss constraint remove, and which does gauge equivalence remove?

Answer. The projection is (3,−1,0)(3,-1,0)(3,−1,0). The constraint removes the third momentum component. Gauge equivalence removes the third potential component.

Self-check. For annulus data, find the gauge parameter that takes A\AvecA to AT\Avec_TAT​. Why does it preserve the real-field condition?

Answer. Its transform is χ^=i(k⋅A^)/∣k∣2\widehat\chi=i(k\cdot\widehat\Avec)/|k|^2χ​=i(k⋅A)/∣k∣2. At −k-k−k, its value is −i(k⋅A^‾)/∣k∣2-i(k\cdot\overline{\widehat\Avec})/|k|^2−i(k⋅A)/∣k∣2, the complex conjugate of its value at kkk.

Self-check. For a real nonzero Fourier mode, should the bilinear canonical bracket pair A^(k)\widehat A(k)A(k) with Π^(k)\widehat\Pi(k)Π(k) or with Π^(−k)\widehat\Pi(-k)Π(−k)?

Answer. It pairs opposite wave vectors. The tested kernel contains δ(k+k′)\delta(k+k')δ(k+k′), as follows from the two forward Fourier phases. A conjugated Hilbert inner product has a different frequency bookkeeping rule.

Self-check. On T3\torus^3T3, can a periodic real gauge parameter remove a nonzero constant potential? What is the symplectic coefficient for each constant pair?

Answer. No. Its gradient has zero mean, so the constant potential is unchanged. Each pair has coefficient vol⁡(T3)=(2π)3\operatorname{vol}(\torus^3)=(2\pi)^3vol(T3)=(2π)3 in the canonical form.

Summary

For vacuum annulus-supported Schwartz data, the Gauss constraint sets the longitudinal momentum to zero and gauge equivalence removes the longitudinal potential. Their quotient has the nondegenerate canonical form on (AT,ΠT)(\Avec_T,\boldsymbol\Pi_T)(AT​,ΠT​) and the smeared bracket ∫f⋅PTg\int f\cdot\PT g∫f⋅PT​g. At nonzero kkk, the projector has rank two. The tested Fourier bracket contains δ(k+k′)\delta(k+k')δ(k+k′). On the torus, three constant canonical pairs survive periodic gauge transformations. The projector itself is bounded on all of L2L^2L2.
