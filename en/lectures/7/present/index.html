Lecture 7

Canonical field observables

We define functional derivatives on rapidly decreasing data, prove the field bracket’s Jacobi identity and recover Klein–Gordon evolution from a Hamiltonian. We identify the charge generating a spatial translation.

An oscillator has a position and a conjugate momentum. A scalar field has one such pair at every spatial point. We give this statement a precise meaning by integrating fields against test functions and defining a bracket on differentiable functions of the initial data.

7.5 Point values fall outside this class

Summary

On real Schwartz pairs, admissible functionals have smooth Schwartz-valued functional derivatives. Their canonical bracket is closed and satisfies Jacobi because the Hessians are symmetric and J\mathbb JJ is constant. The Klein–Gordon Hamiltonian gives ϕt=π\phi_t=\piϕt​=π and πt=Δϕ−m2ϕ\pi_t=\Delta\phi-m^2\phiπt​=Δϕ−m2ϕ. The lower-index translation charge generates ∂iϕ\partial_i\phi∂i​ϕ and ∂iπ\partial_i\pi∂i​π. Point evaluation is smooth but is excluded from this bracket class. Lecture 8 describes the corresponding pairing directly on solutions.
