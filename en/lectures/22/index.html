Lecture 22

The critical vortex

What this lecture does

We prove the critical energy bound with its signs and boundary assumptions. We derive the unit vortex’s radial equations and regularity conditions. A smooth trial field shows why the same bound fails away from critical coupling.

A winding scalar phase cannot be made constant through its vortex core. At critical coupling, its static energy can be written as nonnegative squares plus a flux term. We keep the boundary contribution and specify the sector in which that flux has the required value.

22.1 Planar energy and the imposed sector

Take the static model of Lecture 21, independent of the third spatial coordinate, with zero time and third potential components. Its energy is energy per unit length. On the oriented plane dx1∧dx2\dd x_1\wedge\dd x_2dx1​∧dx2​, set q=v=1q=v=1q=v=1 and write Aem=A1dx1+A2dx2\Aem=A_1\dd x_1+A_2\dd x_2Aem​=A1​dx1​+A2​dx2​, Dj=∂j−iAjD_j=\partial_j-\ii A_jDj​=∂j​−iAj​, and B=∂1A2−∂2A1B=\partial_1A_2-\partial_2A_1B=∂1​A2​−∂2​A1​.

For λ>0\lambda>0λ>0, smooth real Aem\AemAem​ and smooth complex Φ\HiggsΦ on R2\R^2R2, the energy is Eλ=∫R2eλ d2x,eλ=12[B2+∣D1Φ∣2+∣D2Φ∣2]+λ8(1−∣Φ∣2)2.\begin{equation} \label{eq:22:energy} E_\lambda=\int_{\R^2}e_\lambda\,\dd^2x, \qquad e_\lambda=\frac12\bigl[B^2+|D_1\Higgs|^2+|D_2\Higgs|^2\bigr] +\frac\lambda8(1-|\Higgs|^2)^2. \end{equation}Eλ​=∫R2​eλ​d2x,eλ​=21​[B2+∣D1​Φ∣2+∣D2​Φ∣2]+8λ​(1−∣Φ∣2)2.(22.1) The critical coupling here is λ=1\lambda=1λ=1.

Recall winding from Topology and Geometry, Part I, Lecture 3. A nonvanishing field on a circle has a phase lift along one traversal. Its phase increment is 2πn2\pi n2πn for an integer nnn, independent of the lift. The circle is traversed counterclockwise.

Definition 22.2 (Admissible winding sector). For a fixed integer n>0n>0n>0, the admissible winding-nnn sector consists of the smooth fields above with finite energy, B∈L1(R2)B\in L^1(\R^2)B∈L1(R2), and ∣Φ(x)∣→1|\Higgs(x)|\to1∣Φ(x)∣→1 uniformly as ∣x∣→∞|x|\to\infty∣x∣→∞. On every sufficiently large circle, the phase loop has winding nnn.

Uniform convergence makes the field nonzero outside a disk, so these loops are defined and have the same winding by radial homotopy. The vacuum behaviour, integrable magnetic field and specified positive winding are hypotheses. Finite energy alone has not supplied them. For example, the constant vacuum has finite energy and winding zero.

Self-check. Does finite energy alone select the positive winding sector and justify removing every large-circle boundary term?

Answer. No. The sector imposes the vacuum asymptotics, winding and magnetic integrability. The proof then produces a subsequence with vanishing boundary current. It does not assert vanishing along every radius from finite energy alone.

Self-check. Evaluate the smooth trial’s energy at λ=1/4\lambda=1/4λ=1/4. Does its unit flux force the critical lower bound at that coupling?

Answer. Its energy is 2π/3+π/8=19π/24<π2\pi/3+\pi/8=19\pi/24<\pi2π/3+π/8=19π/24<π. The extra term in the off-critical completion is negative, so the critical bound does not apply even though the flux is 2π2\pi2π.

Summary

At q=v=λ=1q=v=\lambda=1q=v=λ=1, the energy density is two nonnegative squares plus B/2+curl⁡KB/2+\operatorname{curl}KB/2+curlK. In the stated asymptotic sector, finite covariant-gradient energy supplies a sequence of large circles where the boundary current vanishes. The winding and integrable magnetic field give flux 2πn2\pi n2πn. Thus E≥πnE\ge\pi nE≥πn, with equality exactly for the Bogomolny equations. The unit radial ansatz uses the one-form αdθ\alpha\dd\thetaαdθ and has the stated regular core expansions. Existence is cited from Taubes. The bound need not hold away from critical coupling.
