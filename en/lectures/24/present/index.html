Lecture 24

The Lax pair of KdV

We prove the Lax identity by applying both compositions to a test function. We state the cited existence and decay results that supply smooth Schwartz solutions. We distinguish this differential identity from a spectral theorem.

Lecture 23 obtained KdV from a Hamiltonian. The same equation can be written as the evolution of a differential operator. We compute the commutator that gives this description and specify the solution class used in the following scattering lectures.

24.4 The boundary of the operator statement

The test-function calculation also gives formal symmetry properties. For compactly supported smooth complex fff and ggg, integration by parts uses the endpoint primitives f‾Lg−Lf‾g=D(f′‾g−f‾g′),f‾Bg+Bf‾g=D ⁣[−4(f‾g′′−f′‾g′+f′′‾g)+6uf‾g].\begin{aligned} \overline f Lg-\overline{Lf}g &=D(\overline{f'}g-\overline f g'),\\ \overline f Bg+\overline{Bf}g &=D\!\left[-4(\overline f g''-\overline{f'}g'+\overline{f''}g) +6u\overline f g\right]. \end{aligned}f​Lg−Lf​gf​Bg+Bf​g​=D(f′​g−f​g′),=D[−4(f​g′′−f′​g′+f′′​g)+6uf​g].​ Both primitives vanish outside a compact set. They do not identify closed operator domains or construct an evolution operator on L2L^2L2. The operators contain derivatives and are unbounded on L2L^2L2. Self-check. Why does Schwartz membership at every separate time not state the full standing hypothesis? What supplies the stronger conclusion here?

Answer. Separate membership does not assert continuity or uniform compact-time bounds in any Schwartz seminorm. Cited continuous Sobolev and weighted norms, applied to time differences in (24.6), give that continuity. Compactness then gives the uniform bounds.

Self-check. Does the proved Lax identity establish constancy of every eigenvalue of an unbounded operator on L2L^2L2?

Answer. No. The proof takes place on a common test-function domain. A spectral conclusion also requires control of operator domains and of the evolution or differentiability of the relevant eigenfunctions. Those analytic conclusions do not follow from the algebra alone.

Summary

For L=−∂x2+uL=-\partial_x^2+uL=−∂x2​+u and B=−4∂x3+6u∂x+3uxB=-4\partial_x^3+6u\partial_x+3u_xB=−4∂x3​+6u∂x​+3ux​, the commutator on compactly supported smooth tests is multiplication by 6uux−uxxx6uu_x-u_{xxx}6uux​−uxxx​. Thus Lt=[B,L]L_t=[B,L]Lt​=[B,L] is equivalent to KdV for smooth uuu. Cited Sobolev existence and weighted persistence, together with the displayed interpolation estimate, supply C∞(R;S)C^\infty(\R;\mathcal S)C∞(R;S) solutions for real Schwartz data. The test-function identity alone is not an isospectrality theorem.
