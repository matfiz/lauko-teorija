Lecture 19

Noether’s second theorem

We derive the formal-adjoint Noether identity for finite-order gauge operators. We apply it to pure Yang–Mills theory and compute Maxwell charge as an outward surface flux.

A symmetry depending on an arbitrary function forces relations between field equations. Maxwell’s identity in Lecture 9 is one example. We prove the general relation and identify its covariant form for a noncommuting gauge field.

19.1 Gauge parameters and compact variations

Let UUU be the interior of a compact region D⊂R1+dD\subset\R^{1+d}D⊂R1+d. Let ϕ=(ϕ1,…,ϕN)\phi=(\phi^1,\ldots,\phi^N)ϕ=(ϕ1,…,ϕN) be smooth on a neighbourhood of DDD. Assume its compact first variation has smooth finite-order Euler expressions Ea[ϕ]E_a[\phi]Ea​[ϕ]: δSD[ϕ]h=∫DEa[ϕ]ha vol(h∈Cc∞(U;RN)).\delta\action_D[\phi]h=\int_D E_a[\phi]h^a\,\vol \qquad\bigl(h\in C_c^\infty(U;\R^N)\bigr).δSD​[ϕ]h=∫D​Ea​[ϕ]havol(h∈Cc∞​(U;RN)). These expressions may depend on derivatives of any finite order. For a multi-index I=(I0,…,Id)I=(I_0,\ldots,I_d)I=(I0​,…,Id​), write ∣I∣=∑Iμ|I|=\sum I_\mu∣I∣=∑Iμ​ and ∂I=∂0I0⋯∂dId\partial_I=\partial_0^{I_0}\cdots\partial_d^{I_d}∂I​=∂0I0​​⋯∂dId​​.

Definition 19.1 (Gauge operator and gauge symmetry). For a fixed integer sR≥0s_R\ge0sR​≥0, a gauge operator at ϕ\phiϕ is a linear map Rϕ:Cc∞(U;Rr)→Cc∞(U;RN)R_\phi:C_c^\infty(U;\R^r)\to C_c^\infty(U;\R^N)Rϕ​:Cc∞​(U;Rr)→Cc∞​(U;RN) given by Rϕ(ε)a=∑∣I∣≤sRRαaI[ϕ]∂Iεα.\begin{equation} \label{eq:19:operator} R_\phi(\varepsilon)^a =\sum_{|I|\le s_R}R^{aI}_\alpha[\phi]\partial_I\varepsilon^\alpha. \end{equation}Rϕ​(ε)a=∣I∣≤sR​∑​RαaI​[ϕ]∂I​εα.(19.1) Its smooth coefficients may depend on the point, the field and finitely many field derivatives. The variations are a gauge symmetry if δSD[ϕ]Rϕ(ε)=0\delta\action_D[\phi]R_\phi(\varepsilon)=0δSD​[ϕ]Rϕ​(ε)=0 for every compactly supported parameter ε\varepsilonε, at every smooth field.

The coefficients are evaluated at the fixed field when the operator acts on the parameter. Their dependence on the field does not remove their ordinary coordinate derivatives in integration by parts. The compact parameter and its derivatives keep the variation away from the boundary of DDD.

19.2 The formal adjoint identity

Definition 19.2 (Formal adjoint and Noether identity). The formal adjoint of (19.1), at the fixed field, is (Rϕ∗E)α=∑∣I∣≤sR(−1)∣I∣∂I(RαaI[ϕ]Ea).\begin{equation} \label{eq:19:adjoint} (R_\phi^*E)_\alpha =\sum_{|I|\le s_R}(-1)^{|I|}\partial_I\bigl(R^{aI}_\alpha[\phi]E_a\bigr). \end{equation}(Rϕ∗​E)α​=∣I∣≤sR​∑​(−1)∣I∣∂I​(RαaI​[ϕ]Ea​).(19.2) A differential relation among Euler expressions holding before imposing E=0E=0E=0 is a Noether identity.

For example, in one coordinate, R(ε)=a(x)εxR(\varepsilon)=a(x)\varepsilon_xR(ε)=a(x)εx​ has R∗E=−∂x(aE)R^*E=-\partial_x(aE)R∗E=−∂x​(aE), including the term −axE-a_xE−ax​E. Formal adjunction holds the field fixed but differentiates the evaluated coefficient as a function of the point.

Theorem 19.3 (Noether’s second theorem). Under the preceding smoothness and first-variation hypotheses, a gauge symmetry gives the off-shell identity Rϕ∗(E[ϕ])(x)=0(x∈U)\begin{equation} \label{eq:19:noetheridentity} R_\phi^*(E[\phi])(x)=0\qquad(x\in U) \end{equation}Rϕ∗​(E[ϕ])(x)=0(x∈U)(19.3) for every smooth field. This identity is asserted on UUU. No first-order assumption on the action or its Euler expressions is required.

Proof. Fix the field and move each parameter derivative by coordinate integration by parts. For each multi-index, repeated one-dimensional FTC gives ∫DEaRαaI∂Iεα vol=(−1)∣I∣∫D∂I(EaRαaI)εα vol.\int_D E_a R^{aI}_\alpha\partial_I\varepsilon^\alpha\,\vol =(-1)^{|I|}\int_D \partial_I(E_aR^{aI}_\alpha)\varepsilon^\alpha\,\vol.∫D​Ea​RαaI​∂I​εαvol=(−1)∣I∣∫D​∂I​(Ea​RαaI​)εαvol. At every step the discarded total derivative contains a derivative of ε\varepsilonε as a factor and has compact support in UUU. Extend it by zero and integrate over R1+d\R^{1+d}R1+d. Fubini and the ordinary fundamental theorem make its integral zero. No boundary regularity theorem for DDD is needed. Summing the finite list of terms proves the pairing identity 0=δSD[ϕ]Rϕ(ε)=∫DEaRϕ(ε)a vol=∫D(Rϕ∗E)αεα vol.0=\delta\action_D[\phi]R_\phi(\varepsilon) =\int_D E_aR_\phi(\varepsilon)^a\,\vol =\int_D(R_\phi^*E)_\alpha\varepsilon^\alpha\,\vol.0=δSD​[ϕ]Rϕ​(ε)=∫D​Ea​Rϕ​(ε)avol=∫D​(Rϕ∗​E)α​εαvol. The parameter components are arbitrary smooth compact tests. The smooth-test fundamental lemma of Lecture 9 therefore gives (Rϕ∗E)α=0(R_\phi^*E)_\alpha=0(Rϕ∗​E)α​=0 on UUU for every component. The field was arbitrary, so this is an off-shell identity. 

The theorem concerns infinitesimal invariance under arbitrary functions. It does not require the field-dependent coefficients to be constant, nor a finite-dimensional parameter space as in Lecture 2.

19.3 The pure Yang–Mills identity

We now work on R1+3\R^{1+3}R1+3. We recall the needed matrix conventions from Topology and Geometry, Part II, Lectures 21 and 23, and verify the identities used here. Let A=AμdxμA=A_\mu\dd x^\muA=Aμ​dxμ be a smooth one-form with values in su(nc)\mathfrak{su}(n_c)su(nc​), the anti-Hermitian traceless nc×ncn_c\times n_cnc​×nc​ matrices, where nc≥2n_c\ge2nc​≥2. The curvature, covariant derivative and real invariant pairing are Fμν=∂μAν−∂νAμ+[Aμ,Aν],DμX=∂μX+[Aμ,X],⟨X,Y⟩=−2tr⁡(XY).\begin{equation} \label{eq:19:dictionary} \begin{split} F_{\mu\nu}&=\partial_\mu A_\nu-\partial_\nu A_\mu+[A_\mu,A_\nu],\\ D_\mu X&=\partial_\mu X+[A_\mu,X],\qquad \langle X,Y\rangle=-2\tr(XY). \end{split} \end{equation}Fμν​Dμ​X​=∂μ​Aν​−∂ν​Aμ​+[Aμ​,Aν​],=∂μ​X+[Aμ​,X],⟨X,Y⟩=−2tr(XY).​(19.4) Here [X,Y]=XY−YX[X,Y]=XY-YX[X,Y]=XY−YX. In form notation F=dA+A∧AF=\dd A+A\wedge AF=dA+A∧A; matrix multiplication accompanies the wedge product. On forms, Dω=dxμ∧(∂μω+[Aμ,ω])D\omega=\dd x^\mu\wedge(\partial_\mu\omega+[A_\mu,\omega])Dω=dxμ∧(∂μ​ω+[Aμ​,ω]), with the commutator taken in each coefficient. The metric and orientation are the Minkowski ones of Lecture 9.

The pairing is nondegenerate because ⟨X,X⟩=2tr⁡(X†X)>0\langle X,X\rangle=2\tr(X^\dagger X)>0⟨X,X⟩=2tr(X†X)>0 for X≠0X\ne0X=0. In a real orthonormal basis for this pairing, it is the sum of products of real components. Thus the component form of Theorem 19.3 applies to these matrix-valued fields and parameters. Trace cyclicity gives ⟨[A,X],Y⟩+⟨X,[A,Y]⟩=0\langle[A,X],Y\rangle+\langle X,[A,Y]\rangle=0⟨[A,X],Y⟩+⟨X,[A,Y]⟩=0. Thus the product rule takes the covariant form ∂μ⟨X,Y⟩=⟨DμX,Y⟩+⟨X,DμY⟩.\partial_\mu\langle X,Y\rangle =\langle D_\mu X,Y\rangle+\langle X,D_\mu Y\rangle.∂μ​⟨X,Y⟩=⟨Dμ​X,Y⟩+⟨X,Dμ​Y⟩. For compactly supported parameters this makes the formal adjoint of R(ε)μ=DμεR(\varepsilon)_\mu=D_\mu\varepsilonR(ε)μ​=Dμ​ε equal to R∗E=−DμEμR^*E=-D_\mu E^\muR∗E=−Dμ​Eμ.

Fix a coupling g>0g>0g>0. The pure Yang–Mills action is SD[A]=−14g2∫D⟨Fμν,Fμν⟩ vol.\begin{equation} \label{eq:19:YMaction} \action_D[A]=-\frac1{4g^2}\int_D \langle F_{\mu\nu},F^{\mu\nu}\rangle\,\vol. \end{equation}SD​[A]=−4g21​∫D​⟨Fμν​,Fμν⟩vol.(19.5) Spacetime indices are raised with η\Minkowskiη. Under a compact variation aμa_\muaμ​, direct differentiation gives δFμν=Dμaν−Dνaμ\delta F_{\mu\nu}=D_\mu a_\nu-D_\nu a_\muδFμν​=Dμ​aν​−Dν​aμ​. Antisymmetry and the covariant product rule then give δSD[A]a=−1g2∫D⟨Dμaν,Fμν⟩ vol=1g2∫D⟨aν,DμFμν⟩ vol,Eν=1g2DμFμν.\begin{equation} \label{eq:19:YMEuler} \begin{split} \delta\action_D[A]a &=-\frac1{g^2}\int_D\langle D_\mu a_\nu,F^{\mu\nu}\rangle\,\vol\\ &=\frac1{g^2}\int_D\langle a_\nu,D_\mu F^{\mu\nu}\rangle\,\vol, \qquad E^\nu=\frac1{g^2}D_\mu F^{\mu\nu}. \end{split} \end{equation}δSD​[A]a​=−g21​∫D​⟨Dμ​aν​,Fμν⟩vol=g21​∫D​⟨aν​,Dμ​Fμν⟩vol,Eν=g21​Dμ​Fμν.​(19.6) The discarded divergence is −g−2∂μ⟨aν,Fμν⟩-g^{-2}\partial_\mu\langle a_\nu,F^{\mu\nu}\rangle−g−2∂μ​⟨aν​,Fμν⟩, which has compact support. The equations DμFμν=0D_\mu F^{\mu\nu}=0Dμ​Fμν=0 are written D⋆F=0D\HodgeStar F=0D⋆F=0 in form notation.

The gauge variation aμ=Dμεa_\mu=D_\mu\varepsilonaμ​=Dμ​ε gives δFμν=[Fμν,ε]\delta F_{\mu\nu}=[F_{\mu\nu},\varepsilon]δFμν​=[Fμν​,ε]. To verify this, expand two covariant derivatives and use the matrix Jacobi identity: [Dμ,Dν]X=[∂μAν−∂νAμ,X]+[Aμ,[Aν,X]]−[Aν,[Aμ,X]]=[Fμν,X].\begin{aligned} [D_\mu,D_\nu]X &=[\partial_\mu A_\nu-\partial_\nu A_\mu,X] +[A_\mu,[A_\nu,X]]-[A_\nu,[A_\mu,X]]\\ &=[F_{\mu\nu},X]. \end{aligned}[Dμ​,Dν​]X​=[∂μ​Aν​−∂ν​Aμ​,X]+[Aμ​,[Aν​,X]]−[Aν​,[Aμ​,X]]=[Fμν​,X].​ The variation of tr⁡(FμνFμν)\tr(F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu})tr(Fμν​Fμν) is the trace of [FμνFμν,ε][F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu},\varepsilon][Fμν​Fμν,ε], hence zero. Thus the action has this gauge symmetry, and Theorem 19.3 gives the pure Yang–Mills identity. It can also be checked directly: 2DμDνFνμ=[Dμ,Dν]Fνμ=[Fμν,Fνμ]=0.\begin{equation} \label{eq:19:YMidentity} 2D_\mu D_\nu F^{\nu\mu} =[D_\mu,D_\nu]F^{\nu\mu} =[F_{\mu\nu},F^{\nu\mu}]=0. \end{equation}2Dμ​Dν​Fνμ=[Dμ​,Dν​]Fνμ=[Fμν​,Fνμ]=0.(19.7) The first equality interchanges the dummy indices and uses antisymmetry. For each index pair, FνμF^{\nu\mu}Fνμ is a scalar multiple of FμνF_{\mu\nu}Fμν​ in our diagonal metric, so the final commutator is zero. This is an identity for every smooth connection, without its equations. For coupled matter, the gauge operator also acts on the matter fields; their Euler expressions then enter the Noether identity. Sheet 19 treats that extension.

19.4 Maxwell charge on a sphere

Return to the Maxwell normalisation of Lecture 9. At fixed time, its Hodge table pulls ⋆Fem\HodgeStar\Fem⋆Fem​ back to Exdy∧dz+Eydz∧dx+Ezdx∧dyE_x\dd y\wedge\dd z+E_y\dd z\wedge\dd x+E_z\dd x\wedge\dd yEx​dy∧dz+Ey​dz∧dx+Ez​dx∧dy. Give Sr2=∂Br(0)S_r^2=\partial B_r(0)Sr2​=∂Br​(0) the outward boundary orientation of spatial R3\R^3R3. 19.5 Covariant divergence cannot be replaced by divergence

The Yang–Mills identity says ∂μEμ=−[Aμ,Eμ]\partial_\mu E^\mu=-[A_\mu,E^\mu]∂μ​Eμ=−[Aμ​,Eμ], which need not vanish. For example, in a spatial coordinate patch take su(2)\mathfrak{su}(2)su(2) generators Ta=−iσa/2T_a=-\ii\sigma_a/2Ta​=−iσa​/2, with [T1,T2]=T3[T_1,T_2]=T_3[T1​,T2​]=T3​ cyclically, and static potentials A1=T1A_1=T_1A1​=T1​, A2=xT2A_2=xT_2A2​=xT2​, A0=A3=0A_0=A_3=0A0​=A3​=0. For g=1g=1g=1, direct differentiation gives E1=−x2T1,E2=−xT2+2T3,∂μEμ=−2xT1.E^1=-x^2T_1,\qquad E^2=-xT_2+2T_3, \qquad \partial_\mu E^\mu=-2xT_1.E1=−x2T1​,E2=−xT2​+2T3​,∂μ​Eμ=−2xT1​. The commutator [A2,E2]=2xT1[A_2,E^2]=2xT_1[A2​,E2]=2xT1​ restores the zero covariant divergence. This is a local off-shell example, not a finite-energy assertion on all space.

The temporal Euler equation gives the non-abelian Gauss law. With Πi=g−2F0i\Pi_i=g^{-2}F_{0i}Πi​=g−2F0i​, the diagonal metric gives E0=DiΠi=∂iΠi+[Ai,Πi]=0.\begin{equation} \label{eq:19:Gauss} E^0=D_i\Pi_i=\partial_i\Pi_i+[A_i,\Pi_i]=0. \end{equation}E0=Di​Πi​=∂i​Πi​+[Ai​,Πi​]=0.(19.9) The commutator must be retained. Lecture 20 studies its Hamiltonian evolution.

Summary

For a finite-order parameter-dependent symmetry, repeated coordinate integration by parts gives R∗E=0R^*E=0R∗E=0 off shell. Derivatives act on the operator’s evaluated coefficients as well as on EEE. For pure Yang–Mills theory the formal adjoint is the negative covariant divergence, giving DμDνFνμ=0D_\mu D_\nu F^{\nu\mu}=0Dμ​Dν​Fνμ=0. Maxwell charge is the outward flux of ⋆Fem\HodgeStar\Fem⋆Fem​ through a large sphere. The non-abelian Gauss equation contains a commutator, so ordinary divergence alone does not express the constraint.
