Lecture 10

Maxwell initial data

What this lecture does

We construct a smooth Maxwell potential from a conserved compact source and compatible compact initial data. We prove propagation of the gauge condition and exhibit the remaining gauge freedom in a vacuum packet.

The Maxwell equations admit many potentials for the same field. We impose a condition on the potential that turns its equations into wave equations. Two compatibility conditions on the initial data make this condition persist during evolution.

10.4 Spatial transversality alone is insufficient

The condition div⁡A=0\divergence\Avec=0divA=0 is often called spatial transversality. It does not impose the scalar condition or Gauss’s law. Self-check. The retarded source field may have nonzero data at time zero. What goes wrong if the homogeneous field is assigned the original data aaa and bbb without correction?

Answer. The sum then has data a+R(0)a+R(0)a+R(0) and b+Rt(0)b+R_t(0)b+Rt​(0). Its wave equation is correct, but its data are wrong. Subtracting both retarded data gives the required aaa and bbb.

Self-check. Why does compact spacetime support, together with conservation, force zero total charge? Does conservation alone force it?

Answer. Conservation makes the total charge constant, and compact time support makes it zero sufficiently early. Conservation alone allows a nonzero constant charge when the spatial integral and vanishing boundary flux are defined.

Self-check. A smooth gauge function satisfies □χ≠0\dAlembert\chi\ne0□χ=0. Does it preserve the electric and magnetic fields? Does it preserve Lorenz gauge?

Summary

A smooth conserved current of compact spacetime support and compatible smooth compact potential data determine a unique Lorenz-gauge Maxwell potential. The construction adds a retarded source field to a homogeneous wave with corrected initial data. The Lorenz function obeys the homogeneous wave equation. Its zero displacement and velocity are the Lorenz and Gauss compatibility conditions. Residual gauge waves preserve the fields while changing potential data. Spatial transversality alone supplies neither condition.
