Lecture 21

The abelian Higgs mechanism

What this lecture does

We use local amplitude and phase coordinates to split the quadratic action into a radial scalar and a massive vector. We count the vector’s independent components and examine the zero-coupling limit and the obstruction at a vortex.

A Maxwell field has two propagating polarisations. A massive vector has three. Coupling the two real components of a complex scalar to the gauge potential supplies the additional mode without changing the total count. We derive this statement near a nonzero scalar vacuum.

21.5 A vortex zero obstructs a smooth unitary gauge

A nonzero complex field has a real phase only locally in general. Around a zero, phase winding can prevent a global branch. Consider the local planar model Φ(x,y)=x+iy\Higgs(x,y)=x+\ii yΦ(x,y)=x+iy. On a circle about the origin its phase winds once, as shown in Figure 21.1. This is a model of the zero, not a claim of finite energy for that profile on the whole plane.

On the punctured plane the circle-valued gauge function g(x,y)=x−iyx2+y2g(x,y)=\frac{x-\ii y}{\sqrt{x^2+y^2}}g(x,y)=x2+y2​x−iy​ is smooth and makes gΦ=x2+y2g\Higgs=\sqrt{x^2+y^2}gΦ=x2+y2​ positive. On each phase patch it is eiqχe^{\ii q\chi}eiqχ with χ=−arg⁡(x+iy)/q\chi=-\arg(x+\ii y)/qχ=−arg(x+iy)/q. It has no single-valued real logarithm on the punctured plane, since its phase winds once around the origin. Any unit-modulus function uuu with uΦ∈R>0u\Higgs\in\R_{>0}uΦ∈R>0​ must equal Φ‾/∣Φ∣=g\overline\Higgs/|\Higgs|=gΦ/∣Φ∣=g, so no alternative unitary gauge avoids this obstruction. The function ggg cannot extend continuously through the zero: along the positive and negative xxx axes its limits are 111 and −1-1−1. The associated potential transformation contains −q−1darg⁡(x+iy)-q^{-1}\dd\arg(x+\ii y)−q−1darg(x+iy), which is singular at the origin. Consequently a circle-valued unitary gauge on the punctured plane does not supply a smooth unitary gauge through a vortex zero. More generally, a smooth circle-valued extension through a zero is contained in a small phase arc on a sufficiently small disk, by continuity. It therefore has a real phase there and zero winding on small circles. At a vortex zero with nonzero phase winding, the forced unitary gauge has the opposite nonzero winding, a contradiction. Lecture 22 keeps the phase winding and the core rather than applying (21.3) globally.

Summary

With kinetic coefficient 1/21/21/2 and potential coefficient λ/8\lambda/8λ/8, the local Higgs expansion gives radial mass λ v\sqrt\lambda\,vλ​v and vector mass qvqvqv. The gauge-invariant vector obeys Proca’s divergence constraint and has three polarisations. Its quadratic constraint pair is second class, leaving three spatial canonical pairs and one radial scalar mode. The original phase variables have a regular q→0q\to0q→0 limit with a massless phase. A vortex zero prevents extending a local unitary gauge smoothly through its core.
