The first-variation formula separates the field equation from a divergence. A continuous symmetry gives another expression for the same variation. Comparing the expressions produces a local conservation law. Integrating that law requires a separate condition on the spatial boundary.

2.4 From a local law to a charge

Answer. Its divergence is zero, but ∫Rj0 dx=∞\int_\R j^0\,\dd x=\infty∫R​j0dx=∞. The integrability hypothesis fails, so this current has no charge as defined here.

Summary

A variational symmetry satisfies δQL=∂μBμ\delta_Q\Lag=\partial_\mu B^\muδQ​L=∂μ​Bμ for arbitrary fields. Its current is jμ=paμQa−Bμj^\mu=\momcurrent{\mu}_aQ^a-B^\mujμ=paμ​Qa−Bμ, and ∂μjμ=−EaQa\partial_\mu j^\mu=-\EL_aQ^a∂μ​jμ=−Ea​Qa. On solutions the divergence vanishes. The integrated charge is constant on the torus, or on Rd\R^dRd under the integrability conditions of Section 2.4. Translations give the stress tensor studied in Lecture 3.
