Summary

For m≥0m\ge0m≥0, data in H1(Rd)×L2(Rd)H^1(\R^d)\times L^2(\R^d)H1(Rd)×L2(Rd) determine exactly one solution in C(H1)∩C1(L2)C(H^1)\cap C^1(L^2)C(H1)∩C1(L2) of the distributional Klein–Gordon equation. Fourier multipliers give continuous dependence on bounded time intervals, smooth approximation and conserved energy. At zero mass, energy omits control of the low-frequency L2L^2L2 norm. The sine bound supplies that control for finite time. Lecture 5 uses the smooth approximation to prove finite speed of propagation.
