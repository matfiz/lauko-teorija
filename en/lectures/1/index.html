Local action, local equation

What this lecture does

We define fields, Lagrangian densities and actions on compact regions. We differentiate the action along a variation and keep the boundary term. We prove that stationary fields are exactly the solutions of the Euler–Lagrange equations.

In Theoretical Mechanics a motion is a curve q(t)q(t)q(t) that makes the action ∫L(q,q˙) dt\int L(q,\dot q)\,\dd t∫L(q,q˙​)dt stationary under variations with fixed endpoints. Now put an oscillator at every point xxx of space. Its displacement ϕ(t,x)\phi(t,x)ϕ(t,x) depends on time and on the point. The coupling between neighbouring oscillators brings in the space derivatives of ϕ\phiϕ. Such a function of time and space is a field. Its action is an integral over a region of spacetime. This course asks what a classical field theory is. The first answer, developed in Lectures 1 to 3, is that a field theory is given by a local action.

1.3 Stationary fields and the Euler–Lagrange equations

Summary

The action of a field on a compact region is the integral of a local density L(x,ϕ,∂ϕ)\Lag(x,\phi,\partial\phi)L(x,ϕ,∂ϕ). On a region between two graphs, its first variation is ∫DEa[ϕ]ha vol\int_D\EL_a[\phi]h^a\,\vol∫D​Ea​[ϕ]havol plus boundary terms built from the momentum current. A field is stationary exactly when E[ϕ]=0\EL[\phi]=0E[ϕ]=0. For the Klein–Gordon density this is □ϕ+m2ϕ=0\dAlembert\phi+m^2\phi=0□ϕ+m2ϕ=0.
