Lecture 8

The symplectic form on solutions

We construct the Klein–Gordon symplectic current, prove conservation of its integral on spacelike graphs, and prove nondegeneracy using compact Cauchy data. A finite spatial interval shows why side flux must be retained.

Lecture 7 describes a field by its displacement and momentum on one time slice. A solution contains these data on every slice. We show that their antisymmetric pairing is independent of a compact deformation of the slice. The boundary term in the first variation determines its sign.

8.1 Solutions and their variations

Fix m≥0m\ge0m≥0 on R1+d\R^{1+d}R1+d. The Klein–Gordon equation is (□+m2)ϕ=0(\dAlembert+m^2)\phi=0(□+m2)ϕ=0.

Definition 8.1 (Space of solutions). The space of solutions Sm\mathcal S_mSm​ is the real vector space Sm={ϕ∈C∞(R1+d,R):(□+m2)ϕ=0,(ϕ(0),ϕt(0))∈Cc∞(Rd)2}.\mathcal S_m=\{\phi\in C^\infty(\R^{1+d},\R): (\dAlembert+m^2)\phi=0,\quad (\phi(0),\phi_t(0))\in C_c^\infty(\R^d)^2\}.Sm​={ϕ∈C∞(R1+d,R):(□+m2)ϕ=0,(ϕ(0),ϕt​(0))∈Cc∞​(Rd)2}. A linearised solution at ϕ\phiϕ is the derivative hhh of a smooth family of solutions through ϕ\phiϕ. Here the allowed variations are h∈Smh\in\mathcal S_mh∈Sm​, realised by the family ϕ+εh\phi+\varepsilon hϕ+εh.

Linearity makes the linearised equation identical to the original equation: (□+m2)h=0(\dAlembert+m^2)h=0(□+m2)h=0. Theorem 4.3 constructs the solution of every compact smooth Cauchy datum. Such data have rapidly decreasing Fourier transforms, so the multiplier formula can be differentiated to every order. The resulting solution is smooth.

Theorem 5.3 gives a common compact spatial support on each bounded time interval. More precisely, if a datum is supported in BR(0)B_R(0)BR​(0), its solution vanishes outside ∣x∣≤R+∣t∣|x|\le R+|t|∣x∣≤R+∣t∣. Derivatives also vanish outside this set. These facts justify the integrals below.

We write δ\deltaδ for differentiation along solution families and ddd for spacetime exterior differentiation. For example, δϕ(h)=h\delta\phi(h)=hδϕ(h)=h and δpμ(h)=∂μh\delta \momcurrent{\mu}(h)=\partial^\mu hδpμ(h)=∂μh, since pμ=∂μϕ\momcurrent{\mu}=\partial^\mu\phipμ=∂μϕ for the scalar field. These two differentiations commute because they act on separate variables. All field-space derivatives here can be evaluated on finite-dimensional affine families of solutions.

8.3 A graph and its orientation

Definition 8.3 (Spacelike graph and its pairing). A spacelike graph is a surface Στ={(τ(x),x):x∈Rd}\Sigma_\tau=\{(\tau(x),x):x\in\R^d\}Στ​={(τ(x),x):x∈Rd} with τ∈C1(Rd)\tau\in C^1(\R^d)τ∈C1(Rd) and ∣∇τ∣<1|\nabla\tau|<1∣∇τ∣<1. The pullback can also be read directly from the differential forms. The pullback of dΣ0\surfel{0}dΣ0​ is ddx\dd^dxddx. In dΣi\surfel{i}dΣi​, replace dt\dd tdt by ∂jτ dxj\partial_j\tau\,\dd x^j∂j​τdxj. Only the term j=ij=ij=i survives, and its sign is negative. Theorem 8.4 (Slice independence). For h1,h2∈Smh_1,h_2\in\mathcal S_mh1​,h2​∈Sm​ and t0∈Rt_0\in\Rt0​∈R, let τ∈C1(Rd)\tau\in C^1(\R^d)τ∈C1(Rd) satisfy ∣∇τ∣<1|\nabla\tau|<1∣∇τ∣<1 and τ=t0\tau=t_0τ=t0​ outside a compact set. On a constant slice, with π=ϕt\pi=\phi_tπ=ϕt​, it is Ω=−δΘ=∫δϕ∧δπ\symplecticform=-\delta\presymplectic=\int\delta\phi\wedge\delta\piΩ=−δΘ=∫δϕ∧δπ, where Θ=∫π δϕ\presymplectic=\int\pi\,\delta\phiΘ=∫πδϕ.

8.5 Side flux changes the pairing

Conservation of the spacetime current does not conserve its integral over a fixed spatial subregion. In one dimension, coordinate FTC gives ddt∫αβω0(t,x) dx=−[ω1(t,β)−ω1(t,α)].\begin{equation} \label{eq:8:side} \frac{\dd}{\dd t}\int_\alpha^\beta\omega^0(t,x)\,\dd x =-\bigl[\omega^1(t,\beta)-\omega^1(t,\alpha)\bigr]. \end{equation}dtd​∫αβ​ω0(t,x)dx=−[ω1(t,β)−ω1(t,α)].(8.7) The boundaries x=αx=\alphax=α and x=βx=\betax=β are timelike sides: they extend in the time direction at fixed spatial positions.

For an explicit local example, take m=0m=0m=0, h1=1h_1=1h1​=1 and h2=(x+t)2h_2=(x+t)^2h2​=(x+t)2. These are smooth wave solutions, although their Cauchy data are not compact. Their current and its integral on I=[0,1]I=[0,1]I=[0,1] are ω0=2(x+t),ω1=−2(x+t),ΩI(t)=∫01ω0 dx=1+2t.\omega^0=2(x+t),\qquad \omega^1=-2(x+t),\qquad \symplecticform_I(t)=\int_0^1\omega^0\,\dd x=1+2t.ω0=2(x+t),ω1=−2(x+t),ΩI​(t)=∫01​ω0dx=1+2t. The current is closed, but the side contribution is nonzero. Boundary conditions must control this contribution when a field is restricted to a spatial region, as in Lectures 12 and 27.

8.6 General Cauchy surfaces

A Cauchy surface meets every inextendible timelike curve exactly once. A timelike curve has tangent of positive Minkowski square. For the same smooth Klein–Gordon solutions with compact Cauchy data, the pairing is also finite and independent of any smooth spacelike Cauchy surface in full Minkowski spacetime, oriented by its future normal. This extension is cited, not proved here. It uses compactness of the solution support on general Cauchy surfaces in addition to current closure. See Khavkine, Covariant phase space, constraints, gauge and the Peierls formula (2014), §3.3.3, Lemma 3.11, arXiv:1402.1282. The coordinate proof above establishes the compact graph deformations needed in this course.

Summary

For smooth real Klein–Gordon solutions with compact Cauchy data, θ=pμδϕ dΣμ\theta=\momcurrent{\mu}\delta\phi\,\surfel{\mu}θ=pμδϕdΣμ​ gives ω=−δθ\omega=-\delta\thetaω=−δθ and dω=0d\omega=0dω=0. The future-oriented graph element is (1,−∇τ)ddx(1,-\nabla\tau)\dd^dx(1,−∇τ)ddx. Its integral agrees with the constant-slice form Ω=∫δϕ∧δπ\symplecticform=\int\delta\phi\wedge\delta\piΩ=∫δϕ∧δπ for a compact spacelike deformation. Arbitrary compact Cauchy test data prove nondegeneracy. On a bounded spatial region, side flux can change this integral.
