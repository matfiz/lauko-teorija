About this course

The companion to Classical Field Theory — actions, evolution and geometry.

Actions, evolution and geometry

The course develops classical fields from local actions, through Cauchy evolution and symplectic structure, to gauge theories, nonlinear waves, scattering and gravity. Part I covers free fields and Maxwell theory; Part II treats nonlinear fields, gauge symmetry and integrability.

The complete course contains 28 lectures and 28 exercise sheets, with 98 problems. It assumes multivariable calculus, linear algebra, differential equations, Fourier analysis and the stated geometric prerequisites. Each lecture gives its own hypotheses and identifies imported results.

Reading the notes

The web text and PDFs come from the reviewed English and Lithuanian LaTeX editions. Numbered definitions, results, equations and figures retain their source references. Printed self-checks and their answers remain part of the lecture text.

Proofs, sketches and cited results have different roles. A sketch names the step left out; a cited theorem is used with its stated hypotheses. Exact computational checks detect selected algebraic errors and do not replace mathematical proofs.

Working through the sheets

Every exercise has a question, hint, short answer and worked solution. Try the question before opening the other blocks. Each sheet is downloadable in three editions: questions only; hints and short answers; full worked solutions. The complete collections are available on the exercise-sheets page.

The four interactive field models illustrate specific calculations in Lectures 4, 5, 18 and 23. A numerical display is an example within its stated scope, not an existence or stability proof.

Your progress and privacy

Outcome ticks express your own judgement. They are stored in this browser along with theme and presentation settings. The site does not infer mastery or assign a review schedule from them.

The optional Matfiz profile and learning records are shared across Matfiz courses in this browser. The My progress page lets you download, restore or remove those records. Nothing is sent to a server by these local progress tools.

Sources and corrections

The bibliography collects the readings and original sources used by the lectures. If you find an error, report the lecture or exercise number and the affected step to the course author.

The site is static. KaTeX renders the mathematics with the course macros, and the figures are generated from the course drawings. Search, reference pop-ups and presentation controls support reading the same source material.
